# Ambassador City Launch Checklist

Welcome, and thank you. This is the step-by-step plan you follow to launch mahjong in **{{city}}, {{state}}**. It is built to run from your phone. No spreadsheets, no fees, no exact addresses to manage. You start tables, the site fills the seats, and players meet up to play.

**Your goal in one line:** one ambassador, a few tables, real players, games that keep happening.

The whole thing follows our North Star:

> **Tables Formed → Tables Filled → Games Played → Recurring Tables**

Every week below ends with one clear milestone tied to that path. If you hit the milestones, you are winning, even if the rest is messy. Messy is fine.

---

## How to read this

- Work week by week. Do not jump ahead; the early weeks make the later weeks easy.
- Check the boxes as you go. Each week has **one milestone** that matters most.
- A "table" is one game of four players for a specific day, time, and general area (your area or ZIP, never a street address).
- You never share anyone's contact info. The site handles all of that for you.

**Quick reference (whole plan at a glance):**

| Week | Focus | Milestone |
|------|-------|-----------|
| Week 0 | Set up your link and your first table | Referral link saved + 1 table created on /start |
| Week 1 | Fill table 1, recruit a co-host | Table 1 at 3 of 4 or full + 1 co-host saying yes |
| Week 2 | Lock a public venue, seed more tables | **At least 1 table filled** + venue confirmed |

| Week 4 | Turn a filled table into a standing game | **At least 1 recurring game** scheduled |

## Week 0: Get set up (about 30 minutes)

This is the foundation. Do not skip it. Everything you do later gets counted because of your referral link.

- [ ] Get your **referral link and code** from the team. Email **hello@findmymahjgame.com** if you do not have it yet, or reply to your welcome email. Your code is short and easy to say out loud (for example, `{{referral_code}}`).
- [ ] Save the link somewhere you can grab it fast: a note on your phone, your text signature, your email signature.
- [ ] Open the link once yourself to see how it works. Tables you start through your link are credited to you, so we can see your city growing.
- [ ] Make a tiny "starter list" of people you already know who might play: current students, past students, friends, neighbors, anyone who has mentioned mahjong. Even 5 names is plenty to begin.
- [ ] **Start your first table.** Go to **/start** ("Start a Table") using your link. Pick a day, a time, and your area or ZIP. Add your first name and a phone OR email. You take seat 1 as the host and coordinator.
  - Pick a real day and time you can actually make. A filled table that meets beats a perfect table that never happens.
  - Choose a day about 2-3 weeks out so people have time to claim seats.
- [ ] Copy the table's **share link** (the /t/ page). This is the page where players "Claim a Seat" and watch the seat counter fill.

**Milestone (end of Week 0):** your referral link is saved and **1 table is created on /start**. That is Tables Formed. You are officially live.

## Week 1: Fill your first table and find a co-host

Now you turn that one table into a real group, and you find a partner so this does not all rest on you.

- [ ] Text or email your starter list with the table's share link. Keep it short and warm. (Copy you can use below.)
- [ ] Watch your seat counter. The page shows progress like "3 of 4 players, 1 seat to go," and a **"Need a 4th"** badge appears at 3 of 4. Use that badge moment to nudge one more person.
- [ ] When the 4th seat fills, the site **automatically emails all four players** a coordination note (first names, general area, a reminder to meet in a public place for the first game, and replies route to you as host). You do not have to send anything; just be ready to reply if someone asks a question.
- [ ] **Recruit 1-2 co-hosts.** Ask one or two reliable people, "Would you help me start one more table?" A co-host can start their own table on /start and helps you reach players you do not know.
  - Good co-host candidates: a fellow teacher, a studio regular, a club organizer, an enthusiastic player who knows a lot of people.
  - Share your referral link with them, or ask the team to set up their own code so their tables get credited too.

**Sample message to your starter list (text or email):**

> Hi {{player_first_name}}, I am starting mahjong games here in {{city}} through Find My Mahj, a free service that helps players form real games. I have a table open for {{day}} at {{time}}. Want to grab a seat? Here is the link: {{table_share_link}}. You just add your first name and a phone or email, and the site fills the four seats and sends everyone the details. Hope you can play.

**Milestone (end of Week 1):** table 1 is at **3 of 4 or full**, and **at least 1 co-host has said yes**. You are moving toward Tables Filled.

## Week 2: Lock a public venue and seed more tables

This is the most important week. New groups meet in a **public place** for the first game. Lining up a friendly venue removes the biggest worry for new players and gives you a home base.

- [ ] **Line up a public venue partner.** Good options: a library, a community center, a senior center, a cafe with a big table, or a studio if you have one. Many of these have a quiet room or a corner table and are glad to host a regular group.
- [ ] Call or visit the venue and ask the simple version: "I run a small, friendly mahjong group. Could we use a table here once or twice a month?" (Script below.)
- [ ] Confirm a day and time the venue is open and welcoming. Write it down.
- [ ] **Seed 2-3 tables total.** With your venue in hand, start one or two more tables on /start (or have your co-host start theirs), tied to that location's general area. Aim for 2-3 active tables in {{city}}.
- [ ] Make sure every table you and your co-hosts start goes through a **referral link** so your city's growth is counted.

**Sample venue ask (phone or in person):**

> Hi, my name is {{ambassador_first_name}}. I help run a small, friendly mahjong group here in {{city}}. We meet in public spots so new players feel comfortable. Would you be open to letting a group of four to eight people use a table here once or twice a month? We are quiet, we clean up, and we would love a regular home. Could I ask what days and times tend to be open?

**Why public, every time:** the first game with new people should be somewhere open and easy to find. Never publish or suggest home addresses or exact locations. The site only ever shares a general area, and you keep it that way.

**Milestone (end of Week 2):** **at least 1 table is filled** (four players, coordination email sent) **and your public venue is confirmed**. You now have Tables Filled and a place to play.

## Week 3: Run a first open or beginner-friendly session

Time to get people in chairs. A live session turns online seats into real players who keep coming back, and it is the easiest way to recruit more.

- [ ] Pick a date at your venue for a **first open play or beginner-friendly session**. Keep it relaxed: come learn, come play, come watch.
- [ ] Decide the flavor:
  - **Open play** if your players already know the game. Just set up tables and let them play.
  - **Beginner-friendly** if you have new folks. Plan a gentle intro (a set has 152 tiles; the three suits are Bams, Craks, and Dots; flowers are all interchangeable). Keep teaching light and welcoming. Many of our players are 60 to 85, so go at a comfortable pace.
- [ ] Invite everyone from your filled tables, plus your co-hosts' players. Use the table share links so new people can claim open seats.
- [ ] Point newcomers to **/play** ("I Want to Play"). They can search by town, use their location, or leave a request to be matched into a game. Anyone who joins this way through your city helps your numbers grow.
- [ ] At the session, ask one simple question out loud: "Who wants to do this again?" Note who raises a hand. Those are your recurring players.
- [ ] Bring an extra set or two if you can, so latecomers and watchers can sit in.

**Sample session invite:**

> We are getting together to play mahjong at {{venue_name}} in {{city}} on {{date}} at {{time}}. All levels welcome, including brand-new players. Come play, learn, or just watch. Reply if you are in, or claim a seat here: {{table_share_link}}.

**Milestone (end of Week 3):** **2-3 tables active** and **1 session held** at your public venue. Games Played is now real.

---

## Week 4: Turn a filled table into a recurring game

A single great game is good. A game that happens every month is the win. This week you make one group standing.

- [ ] Pick your most enthusiastic filled table from Weeks 2-3. These are the people who want to keep playing.
- [ ] Propose a simple rhythm: "Same group, same place, every other {{weekday}}?" Monthly or twice a month is plenty to start.
- [ ] Lock the **next two dates** with that group at your venue so it feels real and on the calendar.
- [ ] For each future date, **start a new table on /start** for that group's general area, so each recurring game keeps showing up and any open seat can be claimed by a new player.
- [ ] Ask your co-host to anchor a second recurring group if they have the players for it. Two standing games is a strong, healthy city.
- [ ] Save your venue contact and thank them. A quick thank-you keeps the door open for the months ahead.

**Sample recurring-game message:**

> That was a great game. Want to make it a regular thing? I am thinking same group at {{venue_name}}, {{day}} {{time}}, every {{frequency}}. I will set up the table each time and the site handles the seats. Next two dates are {{date_1}} and {{date_2}}. Are you in?

**Milestone (end of Week 4):** **at least 1 recurring game** is scheduled with its next dates on the calendar. That is Recurring Tables, the top of our North Star. Congratulations, you launched your city.

## Week 5 and beyond: Keep it growing and report results

You have a working city. Now keep tables forming and let us help you do more.

- [ ] Start at least **one new table each month** so there is always an open seat for newcomers.
- [ ] Keep using **/play** to catch people searching in {{city}} and route them into your games.
- [ ] Check in with your co-hosts monthly. Ask: any new players? any group ready to go recurring?
- [ ] Add a second venue if your first one fills up. More public spots means more rooms for more tables.
- [ ] Celebrate small wins with your players. A photo (with permission) or a quick "we played again" note keeps energy up.
- [ ] **Report your results** to the team once a month. Short is perfect. Send to **hello@findmymahjgame.com** with:
  - How many tables you started this month
  - How many filled (four players)
  - How many games actually met
  - How many groups are now recurring
  - Anything you need help with

**Sample monthly report:**

> {{city}} update from {{ambassador_first_name}}: started {{number}} tables this month, {{number}} filled, {{number}} games met, {{number}} groups now recurring. Going well: {{one_good_thing}}. Could use help with: {{one_ask}}.

**A note on persona, if it helps:**
- **Teachers:** your students are your fastest first table. Turn a class into a standing game.
- **Studio owners:** your studio can be the public venue and the seedbed for several tables at once.
- **Organizers:** your network is your superpower; recruit two or three co-hosts early and grow wide.
- **Event hosts:** turn a one-time event into recurring tables by booking the next two dates before everyone leaves.
