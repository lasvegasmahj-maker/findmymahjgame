# Return Visit and Habit Strategy

The most important question in the whole vision: why would a mahjong player come back. A platform people visit once and forget is worth nothing. This is the honest analysis of habit, community, network effects, and the moat, and what actually drives a return visit versus what only looks like it does.

## The hard truth about return visits

The strongest habit is not the website. It is the game. A player who has a standing Tuesday table has a weekly mahjong habit whether or not they ever open the site again. So the platform's job is not to compete with the game for attention; it is to (1) create and protect that recurring game, and (2) give a light weekly reason to check in for the weeks and moments around it. Most "engagement" features (calendars, feeds, content) are weaker than this and should not be mistaken for the real habit.

## Why would someone return weekly

Ranked by strength:
1. They have a recurring game and use us to manage it: confirm who is coming, fill a seat when someone drops, see the reminder. The game is the habit; we are the tool attached to it. Strongest and most defensible.
2. They need a 4th this week. Urgent, specific, high intent. The single best reason a casual player returns, and it creates a filled table every time it works.
3. The weekly local note (newsletter): a five-minute, genuinely useful local update that makes checking in a small ritual. Works for players not yet in a recurring game and keeps everyone warm.
4. New tables and open plays near me changed since last week. A reason to glance, but weaker; only compelling once local activity is real.

## Why would someone return monthly

- To find a new game when life changes (moved, schedule shifted, group dissolved).
- For occasional events (an open play, a tournament, a retreat). Monthly and episodic, not a habit driver, which is why events are a supporting layer, not the center.
- To bring a friend in, or after a lesson. Lower frequency, real but secondary.

## What creates habit

- A recurring commitment with other people. Social obligation to three other players is the most durable habit mechanism we have. Protect and multiply recurring tables above all.
- A predictable weekly touch. The newsletter at the same time each week, short and useful.
- Friction removed at the exact moment of need. "Need a 4th" answered in one tap. The product earns the habit by being reliably useful when it matters.

## What creates community

- The group itself. "Our Tuesday game" is the community unit. The platform's role is to spawn and sustain these groups, then get out of the way.
- Local identity. A city newsletter, local ambassadors, and seeing real local faces and stories make people feel part of a scene, not users of an app.
- Safety and warmth. Older players stay in communities that feel safe and kind. Our safety-first posture is a community feature, not just a policy.

## What creates network effects

- Two-sided local density: more players make tables fill faster, which attracts more hosts, which creates more tables, which attracts more players. Local, not national; density in a city is what compounds.
- Cross-role loops: ambassadors bring players and teachers; teachers bring students; filled tables and "need a 4th" pull in the last players; the newsletter is only valuable if the local scene is active, so activity and the newsletter feed each other.
- The effect is strongest within a city and weak across cities. This is why we win city by city, not nationally all at once.

## What creates a moat

- Habit: recurring games that are part of people's weeks. Nearly impossible to dislodge.
- Trust: the safe, kind reputation with older players, slow to earn, easy for a careless competitor to fail at.
- Local relationships: ambassadors and teachers who vouch for us. Not purchasable at speed.
- Density and data: knowing who plays where and when in a city, and being the comprehensive local source. The sum of won cities.

Money can buy ads and features. It cannot quickly buy habit, trust, or local relationships. That is the entire defensive thesis.

## What is NOT a real return driver (do not be fooled)

- A national event calendar you browse occasionally. Reference, not habit.
- Content and news for their own sake. Nice, but weak; only the locally actionable parts (tables, open plays, need a 4th) drive return.
- Gamification, badges, feeds. Distractions for this audience. The game is the gamification.
- National scale and big numbers. Vanity. A thin national presence has no habit anywhere.

## The implication for what we build

Everything should be tested against: does this make a real local player return this week and play. The recurring table and the "need a 4th" fill are the core; the weekly local newsletter is the cheapest amplifier; everything else is secondary. Build and operate in that order, manually where possible, and the habit (and the moat) will be real rather than decorative.
