# Ambassador Recruiting Playbook

How we turn Oh My Mahjong relationships, and other warm leads, into Founding Ambassadors who start real tables.

## What a Founding Ambassador is

A Founding Ambassador is a trusted local leader who helps mahjong games actually happen in their city. They are first in their city, recognized by name, and supported directly by the founder. They are not employees and they do not pay anything. They help because they already bring people together, and we make that easier and give them credit for it.

The promise we make them:
- You are the founding face of mahjong in your city.
- We give you simple tools and ready-to-use materials so you spend time with people, not on logistics.
- We help your own community: your students get games to join, your members get partners, your events get reach.
- You get a personal referral code, so every table you help create is credited to you.

## The one number that matters

We are not collecting names. An ambassador only counts when they start real tables. Recruit with that in mind: look for people who can and will form tables, not just people who like the idea. North Star for everything: Tables Formed, then Filled, then Played, then Recurring.

## Who to recruit (and what is in it for them)

Write to the person, not the category. Lead with what they care about.

- Teachers (NMJL instructors). What they care about: their students having places to play between and after classes, and filling their own classes. The pitch: "Your students finish your class and ask, now what? This gives them a game to join this week, and it sends new beginners back to you."
- Studio owners. What they care about: foot traffic, recurring bookings, community around their space. The pitch: "Become the home base for mahjong in your area. We send players to your tables and your space, you become the place games happen."
- Organizers (club and community organizers). What they care about: easier coordination, more members, less herding. The pitch: "Stop chasing people to fill a fourth. The site fills seats and emails everyone when the table is set. You just show up and play."
- Event hosts. What they care about: turnout and a reason for people to gather again. The pitch: "Turn a one-time event into a standing game. We help your attendees keep playing together after the event ends."

## Where to find candidates

1. Your Oh My Mahjong contact list. Start here. These are warm, in-person relationships and they are the highest-leverage asset we have.
2. Local NMJL teacher listings and class pages.
3. JCC and community-center activity coordinators.
4. Active-adult and 55+ community activity directors.
5. Library and senior-center program staff who run game days.
6. Facebook groups and local mahjong clubs.
7. Referrals from ambassadors you already signed (ask every happy ambassador for one name in another city).

## How to prioritize

Score each candidate on two things: reach (how many players they can bring) and willingness (how eager and available they are). Sign high-reach, high-willingness people first. A willing teacher with 20 students beats a famous name who is too busy to act.

When choosing between two strong candidates, pick the one in a higher-priority target city (see the Top 25 Target Cities list), but never pass on a genuinely eager person just because of geography.

## The ask

Keep it warm and specific. The full email version is in the Recruiting Email Sequence. The short verbal or text version:

"It was so good to connect at Oh My Mahjong. I am building FindMyMahj to help people find mahjong games near them, and I am choosing a small group of Founding Ambassadors to be the first in their cities. You came to mind for {{city}} right away. It is free, it takes very little time, and it helps your own students and community get more games. Could I send you the details, or grab 15 minutes this week?"

One ask, one call to action: get them to apply at findmymahjgame.com/ambassadors or book a short call.

## Handling common objections

- "I do not have time." Response: "That is exactly why I built this. The site does the coordinating. Most ambassadors spend a few minutes inviting their own players, and the seats fill themselves. I will set you up personally."
- "I am not techie." Response: "You do not need to be. If you can send a text, you can do this. I will walk you through it on a 15-minute call, and there is a big-button page made for exactly this."
- "What does it cost?" Response: "Nothing. It is free for players and free for ambassadors. We are not selling anything to your group."
- "Will this compete with my classes or my studio?" Response: "The opposite. It sends beginners looking for help back to teachers, and players looking for a place back to studios. You become the local hub."
- "Why should I, what do I get?" Response: "You are first in your city, you are recognized as a Founding Ambassador, your own community gets more games, and you get credit for every table you help start."

## The referral-code model

Each ambassador gets a short code at the moment you approve them, in the format FMM-{CITY}-{FIRSTNAME}, for example FMM-BOCA-RUTH. Tables started through their link or code are attributed to them using the referral system already in the product. This is how we measure who is driving the North Star and who to reward. See the Referral Tracking Plan for the mechanics.

## Goals per ambassador

Set the same simple, repeatable goals for everyone:
- Within 2 weeks: start at least 1 table (this is activation).
- Within 30 days: 3 tables formed and at least 1 filled.
- Within 60 days: at least 1 recurring game (a table that plays on a standing day).

These goals are the same in every city, which is what lets the system scale nationally.

## How this scales nationally

The whole motion is one repeatable loop: identify a local leader, make the warm ask, approve them, give them a code and the launch kit, get them to their first table, then help them make it recurring. It is light-touch (a short approval and a short onboarding), it runs from a phone, and it does not depend on the founder being in the city. Target 25 ambassadors in 25 cities in the first 30 days (see the 30-Day Blitz Plan), then repeat the same loop in the next set of cities each month.

## Recruiting do-not list

- Do not oversell. No "guaranteed," no hype. This is a warm, premium, community-first program.
- Do not sign people who cannot or will not start tables, no matter how well known.
- Do not ask for or share player contact information during recruiting.
- Do not promise features we do not have. If someone asks for something new, say "not yet, here is what works today."
