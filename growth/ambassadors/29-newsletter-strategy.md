# Newsletter Strategy

This is the highest-leverage near-term move in the whole wartime sprint, and it is almost free. A weekly local newsletter is the cheapest tool that makes a mahjong player return on a schedule, and we can launch it this week, by hand, using the existing Las Vegas Mahjong email list. No engineering, no new product.

## The counterintuitive priority order

The brief proposes a national newsletter first, then city newsletters. Reverse it. Start with one city newsletter (Las Vegas Mahjong Weekly), because:
- We already have a real Las Vegas email list, so it has subscribers on day one.
- Local content is what makes people return ("a table near me opened," "open play Thursday"). National content is generic and skippable.
- A national newsletter with thin local content in most cities reads as empty, the same trap as the national calendar.
- City newsletters are the unit that compounds: each won city gets one, and the national newsletter later becomes a light digest on top.

So: Las Vegas Mahjong Weekly now. Scottsdale and Boca when those cities have activity. A national edition only once several cities are live.

## Content structure (Las Vegas Mahjong Weekly)

Short, warm, useful, senior-friendly, scannable on a phone. One screen, five minutes to read. Each issue:

1. This week's tables needing players. The "need a 4th" list with a one-tap claim link. This is the action that creates tables filled, so it goes first.
2. New tables forming near you. Fresh tables to join, by area.
3. Open plays and events this week. The manual calendar: what is happening, where, when.
4. A teacher or beginner tip. One small, friendly mahjong tip or a teacher spotlight (supports the teacher ecosystem later).
5. A community moment. A photo or a one-line story from a real game ("the Summerlin group played their 4th week"). This builds belonging and shows the scene is alive.
6. One clear call to action. Start a table, or reply with your area and we will seat you.

Every issue must contain at least one thing a reader can act on this week. A newsletter that only informs gets unsubscribed; one that seats you at a game gets opened.

## Growth strategy

- Seed from the existing Las Vegas list immediately (the asset we already own).
- Grow it through the core product: anyone who uses "I want to play," starts a table, or claims a seat is invited to the weekly (with consent).
- Ambassadors forward it to their groups; each becomes a local distribution node.
- Every real table and open play is a story that makes the next issue worth opening, which is why density and the newsletter feed each other.

## Engagement potential

- The newsletter is the weekly habit loop the platform otherwise lacks. The game is the deepest habit; the newsletter is the habit for the weeks between, and for players not yet in a recurring game.
- Measure engagement by actions, not opens: seats claimed and tables started from the newsletter. Opens and clicks are secondary.
- Keep it weekly and short. Predictable cadence plus genuine local usefulness is what builds the habit.

## Monetization potential (later, not now)

- Once a city newsletter has a real, engaged local list, it is a clean channel for the mahjong-friendly business program: a sponsored "venue of the week," a teacher feature, a local shop. High intent, local, trusted.
- Do not monetize before the list is engaged and the local scene is real. Selling ads into a thin newsletter burns trust, our main moat.
- Revenue is a Phase 2-3 outcome of an engaged list, not a Phase 1 goal.

## Manual first

For 90 days the newsletter is hand-made: the founder (or an ambassador) writes a short issue each week from what is actually happening, and sends it through the existing email tool. No newsletter software, no automation build. This proves the format and builds the habit cheaply. Automate only once it is clearly working and spanning multiple cities.

## Why this is the wartime priority

Of everything in the hub vision, the weekly local newsletter is the fastest to launch, the cheapest, and the most directly tied to the return-visit problem. It can start this week with assets we already have. It is the single best answer to "why would someone come back," and it requires no software. Start Las Vegas Mahjong Weekly now.
