# Teacher Strategy

Design for the teacher ecosystem (directory, profiles, featured teachers, referrals), and the direct question: is this Phase 1 or Phase 2. Answer: Phase 2. Teachers matter a lot, but as a published public product they come after we have proven recurring tables. What belongs in Phase 1 is the relationship with teachers, not the directory software.

## Why teachers matter

- Teachers are a supply engine. Every class produces new players who need somewhere to play. A teacher who sends students to tables is one of our best player sources.
- Teachers are natural ambassadors. They already convene people, they are trusted locally, and they benefit directly when their students have games.
- Teachers create defensibility. Being the place beginners find a trusted local teacher, with safety built in, is a category few competitors will build well.

## Why it is Phase 2, not Phase 1, as a product

- A public teacher directory needs real, verified teachers to be credible. We do not have them entered yet. An empty or placeholder directory hurts trust.
- The directory does not directly create a recurring table. A teacher relationship does. In Phase 1 we want the teacher signing up students for a table, not a listing page.
- We already built the admin workflow to enter and manage teachers (behind the login). The data can be collected now; the public pages launch when we hit the bar (about 20 real published teachers), not before.

So: in Phase 1, recruit teachers as ambassadors and route their students into tables. In Phase 2, publish the directory once it is real.

## The four pieces, sequenced

1. Teacher relationships (Phase 1, now). Recruit teachers from the Oh My Mahjong list and locally. Get them forming or feeding tables. Enter them in the admin teacher workflow as you go. This is relationship work, not product work.

2. Teacher directory (Phase 2, at 20 real teachers). The public list, readable and trustworthy, filtered by city and state, with the ambassador badge. Launch only when it is real and spread across active cities. The launch bar is already wired into the admin tool.

3. Teacher profiles and featured teachers (Phase 2-3). Individual pages (bio, photo, city) once profiles are real and useful. Featured placement becomes a soft monetization and reward lever (an earned benefit for active teachers, and later a paid feature), but only once there is traffic to feature into.

4. Teacher referrals (Phase 2-3). Two directions: teachers refer students into tables (track via referral codes, same mechanic as ambassadors), and we refer beginner players to teachers. This is a clean two-sided loop, but it only works once both sides (players and teachers) have real density.

## What to do now

- Treat strong teachers as priority ambassador recruits (see the Warm Relationship Activation Plan). A teacher with students is a Hot contact.
- Enter every real teacher you meet into the admin teacher workflow so the directory is ready to publish the day we hit the bar.
- Do not publish the public directory or profiles until they are real. Keep them behind the admin login, as they are now.

## Challenge summary

Teacher relationships are Phase 1 and high priority; the teacher directory as a public product is Phase 2 and should wait for real data. Building and promoting an empty directory now would trade our trust moat for a hollow feature. Recruit teachers as ambassadors today, publish the directory when it is genuinely useful.
