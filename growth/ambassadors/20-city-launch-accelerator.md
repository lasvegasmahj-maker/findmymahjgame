# City Launch Accelerator

The system that turns one ambassador into multiple tables, multiple players, and recurring games. This is the engine that, repeated city by city, builds the network. It complements the per-city plans (Las Vegas, Phoenix, Boca) and the City Launch Checklist with the underlying mechanics and the failure-and-recovery playbook.

## The chain we are building

One ambassador, then multiple tables, then multiple players, then recurring games.

- One ambassador: a trusted local leader who can convene people.
- Multiple tables: they start tables in their own circle and across a few neighborhoods, and we add co-hosts.
- Multiple players: their network plus our priority referrals and the want-to-play pool fill the seats.
- Recurring games: filled tables that adopt a standing day and time become the durable asset.

The leverage points: an ambassador's existing network (instant supply of players), co-hosts (multiply tables without multiplying founder effort), a public venue (a reliable place that removes friction), and the recurring ritual (turns one game into a habit).

## The launch playbook (mechanics)

1. Equip: referral code, launch kit, a kickoff call. Identify their year-round players specifically.
2. Seed: start the first table on the call, live. The ambassador takes seat one and invites their closest players.
3. Fill: use their network first, then our priority referrals and want-to-play pool for the last seats. The "need a 4th" badge does real work here.
4. Anchor: lock one public venue (library, community center, senior center, cafe) as the default meeting place.
5. Multiply: recruit one or two co-hosts in other parts of the city; each starts a table the same way.
6. Make it recurring: after the first game plays, immediately set the same day and time for next week. This single step is the difference between an event and a community.
7. Measure and adjust: track on the City Launch Scorecard; help where it is stalling.

## Activation sequence (the first table)

This is the make-or-break. Tie to the onboarding sequence.

- Day 0: approve, give the referral code, do the kickoff call, start table one live.
- Days 1 to 3: ambassador invites their players; first seats fill.
- Days 4 to 7: fill the table to 4 of 4; the coordination email fires; pick the public venue.
- Days 7 to 14: first game plays; confirm it; set it recurring.
- An ambassador who has not started a table by Day 7 gets a personal founder call. Do not let this slip.

## First 30 days (per city)

- Week 1: ambassador signed and activated, table one started, first players invited.
- Week 2: table one filled and played; one co-host signed; public venue confirmed.
- Week 3: a beginner-friendly open session; two to three tables filling; first recurring game set.
- Week 4: multiple tables filled; at least one or two recurring games holding; the city reaches Green on the scorecard.
- Target shape by Day 30 (proof market, Las Vegas): about 10 tables formed, 6 filled, 3 recurring. Off-season launch cities aim lower and bank a base for the fall.

## First 90 days (per city)

- Days 31 to 60: deepen. Three to six recurring games. Two to three co-hosts. The teacher relationships in the city begin to feed the directory. The ambassador starts to run it with light founder help.
- Days 61 to 90: self-sustaining. Recurring games continue without founder pushing. The ambassador recruits the next host themselves. The city has enough density (ambassador plus teachers plus tables) to qualify for a published city page. Begin handing the city to the ambassador as captain.
- The 90-day bar: the city keeps producing recurring games on its own. That is a won city.

## Failure points and recovery

1. Signed but never starts a table (the most common failure).
   - Recovery: a personal founder call by Day 7; start the table together live on the call. If still nothing by Day 14, the ambassador is likely not a doer; thank them and re-recruit. Protect the Activated metric, not the signed count.

2. Tables form but never fill.
   - Recovery: the ambassador is under-inviting. Push the launch-kit invite scripts, turn on priority referrals, and surface the table in the want-to-play pool and the "need a 4th" badge. Help them personally invite five more people.

3. Tables fill once but never recur.
   - Recovery: make the recurring ask explicit and immediate after the first game. Offer to set the standing time for them. A community that plays once and stops is the quiet failure; the recurring step must be a deliberate habit, not a hope.

4. Players drop and the group dissolves.
   - Recovery: keep a one or two deep waitlist per recurring table via the want-to-play pool, and let Need a Sub fill gaps fast (when shipped). Resilient groups have a bench.

5. The ambassador burns out or goes quiet.
   - Recovery: co-hosts are the insurance. Always have at least one co-host per active city so the city does not depend on a single person. Re-engage with a specific, small next step; if they are done, transition their tables to a co-host.

6. Off-season stall (snowbird cities in summer).
   - Recovery: judge the city on base-building (one recurring game with year-round residents plus a primed fall pipeline), not on table counts. Do not chase players who are out of town.

7. Founder becomes the bottleneck (only the founder can make tables happen).
   - Recovery: this means the playbook is not proven, only the founder is. Deliberately step back after seeding; measure ambassador-driven tables separately. A city only counts as won when an ambassador, not the founder, runs the recurring games.

## How this serves the metrics

Every step targets Activated Ambassadors, Tables Filled, Games Played, and Recurring Tables. The accelerator exists to convert one relationship into a self-sustaining local engine, then to repeat it, which is how the national network gets built without the founder having to be in every city.
