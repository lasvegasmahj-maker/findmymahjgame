# Venue Acquisition Blitz

**Find My Mahj Game ,  Wartime Founder Toolkit**
*Pilot market: Las Vegas. Phase 1 focus: libraries, community centers, senior centers.*

The job: lock down 6-10 safe public venues that will host recurring American Mahjong tables in the next 30 days. Venues are your inventory and your habit engine. Get the venue, seed the players, list the table, run the first game. Move fast, lead with relationships, become the place where Las Vegas mahjong lives before anyone else does.

---

## 1. Mahjong-Friendly Venue Criteria

A good venue is a safe, welcoming public place where four older adults can sit, hear each other, and play for two to three hours during the venue's slow hours. Use this checklist to score any venue before you say yes.

### Must-have (do not host without these)

| Criterion | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| **Public, not private** | A library, community center, senior center, cafe, or similar. Never a private home. This is non-negotiable for safety. |
| **Tables that seat four** | At least one square or rectangular table per group, roughly 34-36 inches wide, with four chairs. A standard card or folding table works. |
| **Quiet enough to hear** | You can hold a normal conversation without raising your voice. No loud music, no TV blaring nearby. Players call tiles out loud. |
| **Off-peak availability** | A 2-3 hour window during the venue's slow hours (mid-morning or early afternoon on weekdays is ideal). |
| **Easy to enter and move through** | Step-free entry or a ramp, doors that are easy to open, a clear path to the table. Many players are 60-85. |

### Strongly preferred

| Criterion | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| **Free, close parking** | A lot or street parking within a short, flat walk. Players carry tile sets. |
| **Good lighting** | Bright enough to read small tiles and the NMJL card. Natural light is a bonus. |
| **Restrooms nearby** | Clean, accessible, on the same floor. |
| **Stable, predictable space** | The same room or corner each week, so the group can count on it. |
| **A welcoming staff contact** | One named person (coordinator, manager, activities director) who is glad to have you and will let regulars in. |

### Nice to have

- Water, coffee, or a cafe on site.
- Room to grow from one table (4 players) to several tables (8-16 players).
- Air conditioning that actually keeps up (this is Las Vegas).
- A bulletin board or newsletter where the venue can promote the game to its own members.

### Phase 1 priority order

1. **Senior centers** ,  your exact audience is already in the building, staff are paid to fill activity slots, and the space is built for older adults.
2. **Public libraries** ,  meeting/community rooms are free, quiet, central, trusted, and bookable in advance.
3. **Community and recreation centers** ,  flexible rooms, weekday off-peak gaps, activities staff who want programming.

Cafes and restaurants are Phase 2 (see scripts below for when a great off-peak cafe appears). Lead with the three public, senior-friendly types first.

### Quick red flags (walk away or fix before hosting)

- Only bar-height or round bistro tables (hard for tile play and for seniors).
- Loud, no quiet corner, peak-hour only.
- Steps with no ramp, or parking far away.
- No single staff contact who will own the relationship.
- Any pressure to share players' home addresses or phone numbers. We never do this.

## 3. The Partnership Offer

This is the deal you are offering. It is free to start. Keep it simple and verbal-friendly, then confirm in one short email so both sides remember it.

### The one-line pitch

> We send you mahjong players during your slow hours. You give them a safe, welcoming place to play. No money changes hands. Everybody wins.

### What each side gives

| Find My Mahj Game provides | The venue provides |
|---|---|
| The players. We invite, remind, and grow the group. | A table (or tables) that seats four. |
| A certified instructor to run the first sessions and welcome newcomers. | A quiet-enough corner for 2-3 hours. |
| A public listing of the game (day, time, venue name and public address only). | A standing day and time we can rely on. |
| Reminders and reliable organizing so the group actually shows up. | A friendly staff contact who lets regulars in. |
| Promotion to our local email list and on findmymahjgame.com. | A quick mention to their own members or customers. |

### What it costs

Nothing. No fee to the venue, no fee to players, no contract. We start with a handshake and a confirmed day and time. Either side can pause with a week's notice.

### The safety promise (state this every time)

- Games are held in public places only.
- We publish the venue name and the venue's public address. We never publish or share a player's home address or personal phone number.
- We use first names only in anything public.
- New groups always meet somewhere public and welcoming, never a private home.

### Where this goes later (mention only if they ask "what's the catch")

There is no catch now. As the game grows, we will offer an optional paid "Featured Venue" tier for places that want extra promotion, premium placement, and help running multiple tables. That is later and entirely optional. The weekly game is and stays free for hosting a single table.

## 4. The Launch Process ,  From "Yes" to a Full Table

Run this every time a venue says yes. Target: first session within 7-14 days of the yes.

### Step 1 ,  Lock the day, time, and start date (Day 0)

- Agree on a standing weekday slot during the venue's off-peak hours (mid-morning or early afternoon work best).
- Confirm the exact room or corner, the number of tables to start, and who lets people in.
- Send the one-paragraph confirmation email above. Add the venue and the session to your Airtable (Organizations and Sessions).

### Step 2 ,  Create the listing and the table claim link (Day 0-1)

- List the game on findmymahjgame.com with: venue name, public address, day, time, and "all levels welcome, beginners included."
- Generate the specific table link (findmymahjgame.com/t/CODE) so people can claim a seat.
- Decide your first-session size: aim to fill one table (4 seats) solidly, with a short waitlist for table two.

### Step 3 ,  Seed the players (Day 1-7)

Goal: confirm at least 4 players, ideally 6-8, before session one. Pull from your warmest sources first.

- **Email the list.** Send the seeding email below to your Las Vegas list, filtered to the venue's neighborhood if you can.
- **Personal asks.** Text or call 5-10 people you know will likely come and ask them directly. Confirmed regulars anchor a new table.
- **Ask the venue to promote.** Send them the listing and a one-line blurb for their newsletter, bulletin board, or front desk.
- **Track claims** in Airtable (People, Attendance) so you know your real headcount.

**Seeding email to the list:**

> **Subject:** New weekly mahjong in {{neighborhood}} ,  claim your seat
>
> Hello {{first_name}},
>
> We are starting a new weekly American Mahjong game in {{neighborhood}}, and I would love to see you there. All levels are welcome, including brand-new players. I will be there to help.
>
> **Where:** {{venue_name}}, {{public_address}}
> **When:** {{day}}s at {{time}}, starting {{start_date}}
>
> Seats are limited, so claim yours here: {{table_link}}
>
> Can't make this one? Reply with your neighborhood and I will point you to a game closer to you.
>
> See you at the table,
> Shauna

### Step 4 ,  Confirm and remind (Day before and morning of)

- Send a reminder to everyone who claimed a seat the day before, and a short one the morning of.
- Reconfirm the room and access with your venue contact.
- Prepare a "go bag": 1-2 spare tile sets, a few current NMJL cards, extra reading glasses, name tags (first names only), and a simple sign-in sheet (first name and email only, never home address).

### Step 5 ,  Run the first session

- Arrive 20-30 minutes early. Set the table, greet people warmly by name, hand out name tags.
- Group players by experience. Seat beginners together and coach them; let experienced players run their own table.
- Keep it light and social. The goal of session one is that everyone has fun and wants to come back.
- Remember the basics so newcomers feel guided: American Mahjong uses 152 tiles with Bams, Craks, and Dots; Jokers are wild; the Red dragon pairs with Crak, Green with Bam, and White is Soap; East is the dealer and starts.
- Near the end, lock in next week. Say the day and time out loud, confirm everyone is coming back, and invite them to bring a friend to fill table two.

### Step 6 ,  Close the loop (within 24 hours)

- Update Airtable (Attendance, Sessions) with who came.
- Email attendees a warm thank-you and the standing weekly details, plus the table link to share.
- Send the venue contact a quick thank-you and the headcount. This is how a one-time game becomes a standing one and how a single table becomes several.

## 5. Pilot Market Recommendation

**Start in Las Vegas, and concentrate, do not spread.** In the first 30 days, win a small number of neighborhoods deeply rather than scattering one game across the whole valley. A cluster of full, happy tables in one area becomes the proof and the referral engine for the next.

### Pilot these two venue types first

1. **Senior centers** ,  the audience (60-85) is already inside, staff are paid to program activities, and the rooms are built for older adults. Fastest path to a full table.
2. **Public libraries** ,  free, quiet, central, trusted, and bookable. Every neighborhood has one, so libraries let you replicate the same playbook block by block.

Add **community / recreation centers** as the close third where a library is booked up or a senior center says no. Hold cafes for Phase 2, once you have proof and can pick a genuinely off-peak spot.

### Pilot these neighborhoods first (in order)

1. **Summerlin** ,  affluent, high concentration of active adults 60+, strong library and rec-center presence, and a community that talks and refers. Best odds of a fast, full, premium table.
2. **Henderson and Green Valley** ,  large, engaged older-adult population, well-run senior centers and libraries, easy parking. A natural second cluster.
3. **Anthem and Sun City** ,  active-adult communities built around amenities and clubs, exactly the standing-game, bring-a-friend culture you want. Strong for word-of-mouth growth.

Centennial Hills, Spring Valley, and central Las Vegas are your next wave once the first three clusters are running.

### Why this approach wins

- **Density beats reach.** Several full tables in Summerlin create visible momentum, referrals, and a waitlist that seeds table two and table three. One lonely game in five neighborhoods creates nothing.
- **The audience is already clustered.** Summerlin, Henderson, Anthem, and Sun City have the highest concentration of your 60-85 players in public, amenity-rich settings.
- **The playbook repeats.** Libraries and senior centers exist in every neighborhood and run the same way, so once the launch process works in Summerlin, you copy it east to Henderson and north to Centennial Hills with almost no new thinking.
- **It makes you the source of truth fastest.** Owning Summerlin, then Henderson, then Anthem in 30 days means that when anyone in Las Vegas searches for a mahjong game, the answer is already Find My Mahj Game.

### 30-day target

Aim for **6-10 standing tables across 2-3 neighborhood clusters**, anchored by senior centers and libraries, each with a confirmed weekly day and time and a venue contact who is glad to have you. That is a defensible beachhead and the foundation for owning the Las Vegas market.
