# Mahjong-Friendly Business Strategy

A national partnership program that turns restaurants, coffee shops, libraries, community centers, senior centers, country clubs, and hotels into safe places where mahjong players gather. This is real and valuable, and it is the platform's eventual revenue layer. It is also a Phase 2-3 effort. Doing it now, before we have players to send to venues, would be selling an audience we do not yet have.

## Two different motives, kept separate

There are two reasons to engage venues, and they have very different timing:

1. Venues as supply (Phase 1-2, free, do some now). We need safe public places for the first tables and open plays. This is operational, not a program: an ambassador asks a local library or senior center to host a weekly game. Free, simple, and directly serves recurring tables. Do this now, manually, in launch cities.

2. Venues as revenue (Phase 2-3, paid, wait). A formal partnership program with featured listings, sponsorship, and advertising. This only works once we have a real, engaged local audience to offer them. Selling a "featured venue" into a platform with no players is not credible and burns trust.

Do not confuse the two. Right now we want venues to host a free weekly game, not to pay us.

## Target venues, by near-term usefulness

- Libraries, community centers, senior centers: best for Phase 1. Free or cheap rooms, trusted, safe, and exactly where our older players already feel comfortable. Use them now as the default meeting places.
- Coffee shops and casual restaurants: good for smaller daytime tables; some want the off-peak foot traffic. Useful in Phase 2.
- Country clubs and hotels: Phase 3. Higher-end, slower to partner, better suited to the paid program and to events, retreats, and tournaments later.

## The program (Phase 2-3 design)

When we are ready, the offer to a venue is simple: we send you mahjong players during your slow hours, and you give them a welcoming, safe place to play. Tiers:
- Listed venue (free): appears as a mahjong-friendly place to meet in that city. Builds the directory and goodwill.
- Featured venue (paid or earned): highlighted in the city page and the local newsletter as "venue of the week." This is the clean monetization unit, sold into an already-engaged local list.
- Sponsor (paid): supports the local newsletter or an open play in exchange for tasteful local credit.

Advertising, sponsorship, and featured placement all become credible only after the local newsletter and city pages have real, engaged audiences. Until then, keep venues free and operational.

## Revenue evaluation

- Real but later. An engaged local audience of mahjong players is genuinely valuable to local venues and senior-focused businesses. That is the durable revenue thesis.
- Sequencing: free listings build the directory; the newsletter builds the audience; only then do featured and sponsor tiers have value. Revenue is an outcome of density, not a starting point.
- Keep it tasteful and safe. Our audience trusts us because we are not spammy. Any monetization that erodes that trust costs more than it earns.

## What to do now

- Operationally, in launch cities only: line up one or two free public venues (library, community center, senior center) per city to host tables and a first open play. This serves Phase 1 directly.
- Keep a simple list of friendly venues as you meet them (the start of the future directory), but do not build a program or sell anything yet.
- Revisit the paid program in Phase 2-3, once a city has an engaged newsletter and real player density.

## Challenge summary

The business program is a strong eventual revenue layer and a weak near-term priority. Use venues now, for free, as places to play. Build the paid partnership program later, once we have an engaged local audience worth paying to reach. Selling venue partnerships before we have players would be selling an empty room.
