# Mine Existing Las Vegas Assets

The highest-ROI growth action in the business. We do not need new awareness; we have warm, local players already, in the Las Vegas Mahjong email list, the Airtable CRM (People, Sessions, Attendance, Corporate Leads, Organizations, Instructors), and the lesson-leads sheet. This is the founder execution plan to convert those relationships into real Find My Mahj Game tables.

Rule for the next 30 days: every outreach ends with one ask, start or join a table at a specific day and time. Not "check out the site." A table, a day, a time.

Where the data lives: the full mahjong email list (Drive), the Airtable People table (Lifecycle Stage, Last Activity, Total Sessions), Attendance (who came to sessions), Corporate Leads, Organizations, and the lesson-leads sheet. Pull each segment from there.

Segments are ranked by expected conversion to a real table.

## 1. Existing hosts (highest conversion)

People who already host or run games. In Airtable, anyone tagged as a host or who has organized sessions.

- Outreach strategy: personal text or call. They already convene people; we just give them the tool and credit. Aim to turn each into an ambassador with a referral code on the spot.
- Exact message (text): "Hi {{first}}, it is Shauna. I built a free tool that makes it easy to fill your mahjong table and find a 4th when someone cancels. Could I set up your group on it this week, takes 5 minutes and I will do it with you?"
- Follow-up cadence: call if no reply in 2 days; one more nudge at day 5; then leave.
- Expected conversion: 40 to 60 percent to a formed table.
- Success metric: tables formed and filled by existing hosts.

## 2. Existing organizers and club leaders

Organizers from the Organizations table and anyone who coordinates groups.

- Outreach strategy: call. Position as making their coordination easier (the site fills seats and emails everyone when the table is set).
- Exact message (email): "Subject: an easier way to fill your mahjong games. {{first}}, you do the hard work of getting people together. I made a free tool that fills the empty seats and handles the reminders, so you just show up and play. Can I set your group up this week? I will walk you through it in 15 minutes."
- Follow-up cadence: Day 0 email, Day 2 call, Day 6 final note.
- Expected conversion: 35 to 50 percent.
- Success metric: tables formed by organizers, and how many became recurring.

## 3. Open play attendees (warmest players)

People in Attendance who came to open play. They already show up to play in person.

- Outreach strategy: email or text to the group; invite them to a specific recurring table near them. These people convert because they have a proven habit of showing up.
- Exact message (text): "Hi {{first}}, we are starting a few regular mahjong tables around town so you always have a game. There is one forming {{day}} {{time}} near {{area}}. Want me to save you a seat?"
- Follow-up cadence: send, then a reminder 3 days later to non-responders with a different day option.
- Expected conversion: 25 to 40 percent to claiming a seat.
- Success metric: seats claimed (tables filled) and repeat attendance.

## 4. Current students

Active lesson students (People with recent Last Activity or in a current class).

- Outreach strategy: in person at the next class plus a follow-up message. They are learning and want somewhere to practice. A table is the natural next step after a lesson.
- Exact message (in class, then text): "Now that you know the basics, the best way to get good is to play every week. We are starting a beginners-welcome table {{day}} {{time}}, want to join?"
- Follow-up cadence: ask in class, text the same week, remind once before the first session.
- Expected conversion: 30 to 45 percent (high intent, needs a beginner-friendly table).
- Success metric: students seated at a recurring beginners table, and retention to a second game.

## 5. Former students

People who took lessons in the past (older Last Activity).

- Outreach strategy: a warm re-engagement email. Many stopped because they had nowhere to play. A standing table is the reason to come back.
- Exact message (email): "Subject: your mahjong seat is waiting. {{first}}, it has been a while. The thing most people miss after lessons is a regular game. We now have weekly tables around Las Vegas, and there is room near {{area}}. Want me to get you back to the table?"
- Follow-up cadence: Day 0 email, Day 7 a second email with a specific table and day, then leave.
- Expected conversion: 10 to 20 percent.
- Success metric: reactivated players seated at a table.

## 6. Newsletter subscribers

The full email list. Largest and coldest of the warm pool.

- Outreach strategy: a short, warm broadcast (not a generic blast) with one clear ask and a few specific tables by area. Then route responders to the nearest table.
- Exact message (email): "Subject: find your weekly mahjong table. We are starting regular games across Las Vegas so you always have a table. Tell us your area and we will save you a seat at the closest one. Reply with your neighborhood, or start your own table in one minute at findmymahjgame.com/start."
- Follow-up cadence: send once, resend to non-openers after 5 days with a different subject. Do not over-mail.
- Expected conversion: 2 to 5 percent to a seat or a started table (small percent, large list).
- Success metric: replies by area (feeds the heat map and tells us where to place the next table) and tables formed from the list.

## 7. Corporate participants

People from corporate sessions and the Corporate Leads pipeline.

- Outreach strategy: lower priority for recurring neighborhood tables (corporate is a different motion), but valuable for office or community lunchtime games and for referrals. Personal note to the contact.
- Exact message (email): "{{first}}, your team enjoyed the mahjong session. Many groups keep it going with a standing monthly game. Want me to help set one up for your office or community?"
- Follow-up cadence: one email, one follow-up, then park unless they respond.
- Expected conversion: 5 to 15 percent to a recurring office or community table.
- Success metric: recurring corporate or community tables, and referrals to neighborhood players.

## The founder execution order (this week)

1. Existing hosts and organizers first (calls). Highest conversion, and each one can run a table without you.
2. Open play attendees and current students next (group invites to specific tables). They fill the seats.
3. Then the newsletter broadcast (fills gaps and surfaces demand by area).
4. Former students and corporate as time allows.

Tag everyone you contact in the Warm Relationship Pipeline so nothing leaks. The single number to watch this week: tables filled from existing Las Vegas relationships.
