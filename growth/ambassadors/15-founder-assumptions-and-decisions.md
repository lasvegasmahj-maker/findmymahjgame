## 3. Minimum proof point (define success and failure)

The user requested a clear bar. Here is a proposed definition for the Las Vegas launch. Confirm it or change the numbers; the bar is the founder's to set.

Proposed success by Day 30:
- At least one recurring game that an ambassador other than the founder created and runs, that has played 2 or more times, with players who came through the system.
- Plus the fuller target on track or met: 6 or more tables filled and 3 or more recurring games still playing in Week 4 without the founder pushing them.

The single clearest proof: someone who is not Shauna formed and filled a table that became a standing game. That shows the playbook works, not just the founder.

Proposed failure by Day 30:
- Zero recurring games, or the only real activity is founder-driven (no ambassador independently formed and filled a table). That means we proved Shauna can do it, not that the playbook can.

Decision this forces: if we hit the failure line, we stop and fix the playbook before spending effort on more cities.

## 5. Competitive response

If a well-funded competitor launched the same idea tomorrow, what protects us? These are candidate answers drawn from assets we already have. Your job is to confirm which are real and which to lean on.

Assets we may already hold (confirm which are true and strong):

- The sister-site audience (lasvegasmahj.com) and the founder's credibility as a certified instructor.
- Density of real recurring tables in a city, which is hard to fake and creates a local habit.

Questions only you can answer:
- What is our actual moat, in one sentence?

Decision this forces: pick the moat and concentrate effort there. Money can buy ads; it cannot quickly buy trusted local relationships or a habit of recurring tables. If those are the moat, the ambassador program is not a growth tactic, it is the defense.
