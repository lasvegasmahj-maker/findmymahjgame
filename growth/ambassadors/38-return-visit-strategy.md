# Return Visit Strategy

Why would a player come back. This is the question the whole vision rests on, and it is the test every feature must pass. The deeper version of this analysis is in the Return Visit and Habit doc; this is the sharpened, decision-ready version, with the activity-first lens applied.

## Why a user returns weekly

Ranked by strength:
1. They have a recurring game and use us to run it: who is coming, fill a seat when someone drops, the reminder. The game is the habit; we are the tool attached to it. Strongest and most defensible.
2. They need a 4th this week. Urgent, specific, high intent, and it fills a table every time it works.
3. Something is happening near them, soon, that changed since last week. This is the activity-first payoff: a list of real local activities (open plays, new tables, lessons) that is different every week gives a reason to look. A static venue directory does not change, so it gives no reason to return. This is the core argument for activity-first over a venue directory.
4. The weekly local note (the newsletter): a short, useful, same-time-each-week ritual.

## Why a user returns monthly

- Life changed (moved, schedule shifted, group dissolved) and they need a new game.
- An occasional event (open play, tournament, retreat). Monthly and episodic, a supporting layer, not the habit.
- After a lesson, or to bring a friend.

## What creates habit

- A recurring commitment with other people. Social obligation to three other players is the most durable habit we have. Protect and multiply recurring tables above all.
- A predictable weekly touch (the newsletter) and friction removed at the moment of need (need a 4th answered in one tap).
- Changing local content. Activity-first discovery means there is always something new to check; this is what turns occasional use into a weekly glance.

## What creates community

- The group itself. "Our Tuesday game" is the community unit; the platform spawns and sustains these groups, then gets out of the way.
- Local identity: a city newsletter, local ambassadors, real local faces and stories.
- Safety and warmth, which keep older players coming back.

## What creates network effects

- Two-sided local density: more players fill tables faster, which attracts more hosts and tables, which attracts more players. Local, not national.
- Cross-role loops: ambassadors bring players and teachers; teachers bring students; activities and need-a-4th pull in the last players; the newsletter is only valuable if the local scene is active, so activity and the newsletter feed each other.
- Activity inventory compounds the effect: each new open play or table makes the local list more worth checking, which brings more players, which creates more activity.

## What creates defensibility

- Habit: recurring games embedded in people's weeks. Nearly impossible to dislodge.
- Trust: the safe, kind reputation with older players. Slow to earn, easy for a careless competitor to fail at.
- Local relationships and density: ambassadors, teachers, and a live local activity feed that a scraper cannot replicate, because it is relationships and real events, not a static list.

Money buys ads and features. It cannot quickly buy habit, trust, or local relationships, or a living local activity feed. That is the defense.

## The challenge to the assumptions

- Assumption: more listings drive return. Wrong. Static listings (venues) do not drive return; changing, local, actionable activity does, plus your own recurring game.
- Assumption: a national hub drives habit. Wrong. Habit is local. A thin national presence has no habit anywhere. Win cities.
- Assumption: content and news drive return. Only the locally actionable parts (real tables, open plays, need a 4th) do. General content is weak for this audience.

## The implication

Build and operate so there is always a real, local, actionable reason to come back this week: a recurring game to manage, a 4th to find, fresh nearby activity to see, and a weekly note. Activity-first discovery and recurring tables are the engine of return; a venue directory is not. Test every feature against this.
