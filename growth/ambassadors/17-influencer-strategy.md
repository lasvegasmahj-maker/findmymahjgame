# Influencer Strategy

A blunt frame first: most influencer activity produces traffic, and our goal is not traffic. We judge every influencer by one question, did this create real tables that filled and kept playing. By that test, the tiers are very unequal. Tier A is where the value is. Tier B is a modest assist. Tier C is mostly a distraction in City Launch Mode and should wait.

## Tier A: Community leaders, teachers, studio owners, Facebook group admins, club organizers

These are not really influencers. They are ambassadors in waiting. They already convene players, so they can turn attention into seated tables in days. This tier is the priority.

- Recruiting strategy: treat exactly like ambassador recruiting. Find them through the Oh My Mahjong list, local teacher and club searches, JCC and senior-center coordinators, and the admins of city and regional mahjong Facebook groups. Approach warmly and personally, one at a time.
- Offer: the Founding Ambassador package (status, priority referrals after activation, launch kit, founder access). For Facebook group admins specifically, offer to be the easy answer to the question they get every week ("anyone want to play near X"): a link that forms a real table.
- Outreach sequence: Day 0 warm note referencing the shared connection or their group. Day 2 a short "here is exactly how it helps your members" with the launch kit. Day 5 offer a 15-minute call to start their first table together. Day 12 a gentle final note. Move on if no reply; this pool is large.
- Expected ROI: highest of all tiers. One engaged group admin or teacher can seed several tables in a week. Realistic target: 2 to 5 filled tables per activated Tier A leader in the first month.
- Success metrics: activated Tier A leaders, tables filled and games played attributed to them (via their referral code), and recurring games started. Not follower counts, not post reach.

## Tier B: Mahjong creators, YouTubers, podcasters, newsletter writers, bloggers

Useful for credibility and for reaching players who have no local leader yet, but their audiences are national and diffuse, so attribution to real tables is weak. Treat as a supporting channel, not a growth engine.

- Recruiting strategy: a short, specific list (the handful of real mahjong creators and newsletters). Lead with usefulness to their audience, not a generic sponsorship ask. Offer them something genuinely worth sharing: the free tool that helps their followers actually find a game.
- Offer: early access, a founder conversation or interview, a co-created "find a game near you" segment, and credit as a launch partner. Avoid paying flat fees for a post; if any money moves, tie it to a tracked link and real signups in launch cities.
- Outreach sequence: Day 0 a personal note that shows you know their work and proposes one specific, audience-useful collaboration. Day 4 follow up with a ready-to-use blurb and link. Day 10 final note. Keep it to a few high-fit names rather than a blast.
- Expected ROI: modest and slow. Good for trust and for seeding demand in cities that later get an ambassador. Do not expect direct recurring tables from a single post.
- Success metrics: tracked signups and "I want to play" requests by city from their links (which then feed the heat map and tell us where to place an ambassador), plus any creators who convert into Tier A leaders themselves.

## Tier C: Lifestyle influencers (active aging, retirement, women's communities, brain health, lifelong learning, community building)

Tempting because the audience demographics fit, but intent is low and the path from a lifestyle post to four people committing to a weekly time is long and unmeasurable. In City Launch Mode this is the lowest priority. Do not spend founder time or money here yet.

- Recruiting strategy: none active now. Keep a watch list. Revisit only after the three proof cities are working and we have published city pages and profiles to point people to.
- Offer (later): a feel-good partnership story (mahjong for connection and brain health among older adults), pointed at a specific city that already has tables, so the attention lands somewhere real.
- Outreach sequence (later): partner on one city at a time, only where an ambassador and tables already exist, so attention converts.
- Expected ROI: low and hard to attribute in the near term. Possible brand value later. Not a near-term table driver.
- Success metrics: if ever run, judge strictly by city-level "I want to play" requests and tables formed in the targeted city, with a kill switch if it only produces traffic.

## The honest recommendation

Collapse "influencer strategy" into "recruit more Tier A leaders," which is the ambassador program by another name. Use Tier B lightly for credibility and to light up demand in cities on the heat map. Park Tier C until we have real city pages to send people to. Spending equally across three tiers would scatter founder time against the one metric that matters.
