# Strategic Reset and 30-Day Wartime Plan

Dated 2026-06-09. This document resets the strategy around the clarified vision: Find My Mahj Game becomes the national source of truth for American Mahjong. Table formation is a critical feature, not the business. The next 30 days matter more than the next 12 months. Ranked by impact, speed, defensibility, relationship leverage, and network effects. It challenges the prior plan and supersedes the earlier growth and vision docs where they conflict.

## Core Vision Reset

Find My Mahj Game is the national source of truth for American Mahjong. When a player wants anything mahjong-related (people to play with, open plays, tournaments, retreats, cruises, teachers, mahjong-friendly venues, community groups, travel games, news, newsletters), the first thought is "check Find My Mahj Game." Table formation is the deepest habit you can build, but it is one feature inside a wider promise: easily find people, places, events, and opportunities to play safely.

Honest reconciliation with the prior "recurring tables first" thesis:

**What the prior thesis got right, and keep:**
- Recurring tables are the stickiest behavior you can own. A player in a standing Tuesday game has no reason to leave you. That is real retention, not vanity reach.
- A national directory of "everything mahjong" that is thin and stale is worse than worthless. It signals you are not the authority. Breadth without truth kills trust, and trust is your whole edge with a 60-to-85 audience.
- Local density compounds. Ten active Las Vegas tables produce referrals, testimonials, and word of mouth that a national list of half-dead links never will.

**What changes given the clarified vision:**
- The prior thesis treated the national hub as "far off." That is wrong for a niche this small. American Mahjong is a tiny, tightly networked world where the public events, teachers, and venues largely already exist and are knowable through relationships you already hold. Being first and most complete is achievable in weeks, not years, and in a small niche first-and-complete IS a moat.
- The danger of the prior thesis was using "go deep local first" as an excuse to delay the land grab. If a competitor aggregates the Oh My Mahjong network and public event calendar before you do, you lose the source-of-truth position permanently while you are still seeding tables.
- So the emphasis updates to: do BOTH now. Run the relationship-driven inventory land grab (events, teachers, venues nationally where they are public and known) AND seed Las Vegas recurring tables. The weekly newsletter is the connective tissue: it turns a static directory into a habit and a reason to come back.

The synthesis to carry through every decision below: **inventory breadth is the fastest land grab and the clearest path to source-of-truth; recurring tables are the deepest habit moat; the newsletter is the distribution and return layer that binds them.** Anything that does not serve one of those three in 30 days is noise.

## North Star Review

The current funnel (Tables Formed → Filled → Played → Recurring) is a good depth metric and you should keep it. But it only measures table formation, which is one feature. If the vision is source of truth, a funnel that ignores inventory coverage, reach, and habit will steer all your energy back into the one feature and quietly abandon the land grab. Measure the vision, not just the feature.

**Recommended framework: one primary metric, four supporting dimensions.**

**Primary North Star: Weekly Active Players Helped (WAPH)** ,  the count of distinct players each week who either (a) got matched into a table or attended a listed event, or (b) opened/clicked the newsletter to find people, places, or events. This is the cleanest proxy for "players treat us as the source of truth," because it captures both the deep action (played) and the habit action (came back to look). It rises when inventory is richer, when the newsletter is more useful, and when tables actually form. It does not let you fake progress with a pile of dead directory links.

**Four supporting dimensions (one number each, reviewed weekly):**
1. **Depth (habit moat):** Recurring tables active this week. Keep the full Formed → Filled → Played → Recurring funnel underneath it. This is the stickiest behavior and the hardest to copy.
2. **Coverage (source-of-truth):** Inventory items live per active city = events + open plays + teachers + venues. Track total and per-city, because per-city is what makes you authoritative locally.
3. **Reach (distribution):** Newsletter active readers (opened or clicked in the last 4 weeks). This is your owned channel and the leading indicator of source-of-truth mindshare.
4. **Supply (network effect):** Activated ambassadors and hosts = people who listed inventory or hosted/seeded a table in the last 30 days. Not signups; activations.

Why this set: WAPH is the single number to rally around; Depth keeps you honest that habit beats breadth; Coverage and Reach make sure you actually run the land grab; Supply tracks the network effect that makes all of it self-sustaining. Five numbers, reviewable on one screen, none of them gameable with stale data.

## Competitive Moat Analysis

Forced ranking by defensibility for the source-of-truth vision. No ties.

1. **Oh My Mahjong relationships** ,  A small number of warm ties to the people who run the events, studios, and teacher networks. Aggregate them and you get most of the national inventory and credibility in one move. A competitor cannot cold-email their way into this.
2. **Trust** ,  In a 60-to-85 audience, trust is the entire purchasing decision, it took years to earn, and it is nearly impossible for a new entrant to fake. It makes every other asset convert.
3. **Las Vegas Mahjong audience** ,  An owned, warm email list that gives you instant distribution for the newsletter and table seeding on day one. Owned distribution is durable; rented attention is not.
4. **Teacher relationships** ,  Teachers are the supply hubs who bring players, lend authority, and refer locally; each one is hard for a competitor to peel away once you are their default.
5. **Safety reputation** ,  A specific, credible differentiator (public meetups, no shared contact info) that the target audience cares about deeply and that competitors will be slow to match without the same trust.
6. **Ambassador network** ,  Local activators who scout inventory and host tables; a real network effect, but partly poachable and only as strong as their activation rate.
7. **Newsletter audience** ,  Becomes a strong moat once large and habitual, but today it is a channel you still have to build into a habit, so it ranks below the relationships that feed it.
8. **Event inventory** ,  Most of it is public information, so being first and most complete is an advantage, but it is the most copyable asset on this list and decays without constant upkeep.
9. **Venue relationships** ,  Valuable for grounding tables and events in real places, but currently thinnest, most local, and easiest for a competitor to replicate venue by venue.

The pattern: relationships and trust outrank data and software every time. Defend the top five hardest.

## Las Vegas First

The sequence "own Las Vegas, then win 5 markets, then go national" is **directionally correct, with one important caveat.** Use Las Vegas to prove the deep habit moat (recurring tables) because density only happens locally and you have the home-field assets here. But do NOT confine the inventory land grab to Las Vegas. Inventory is national public information reachable through national relationships, and the source-of-truth position is won or lost nationally. So: **own Las Vegas on depth; run the inventory/newsletter land grab nationally from day one.** If you wait to be national on inventory until you have "finished" Las Vegas, a competitor takes the source-of-truth slot while you are still seeding tables.

"Owning Las Vegas" must be concrete and checkable. Target by end of the proving period:
- **Recurring tables:** 10 standing tables that have played 3+ times (true recurrence, not one-offs). This is the real moat number.
- **Tables formed total:** 25+ formed, to feed the 10 recurring.
- **Events / open plays listed:** 20+ live Las Vegas events and open plays, kept current weekly. Stale entries do not count.
- **Teachers:** 8 active Las Vegas teachers listed and engaged (at least referring players or hosting).
- **Venues:** 5 mahjong-friendly Las Vegas venues confirmed and listed.
- **Newsletter readers:** 500 active local readers (opened or clicked in the last 4 weeks), seeded from the Las Vegas list.
- **Ambassadors:** 5 activated Las Vegas ambassadors (each has listed inventory or hosted a table in the last 30 days), not 5 signups.

If you hit those seven numbers, you own Las Vegas in a way a competitor cannot quietly undo, and you have a written playbook to copy into market two. If you cannot hit them in your home market with your warmest assets, expansion would only multiply a broken model, so this is also your honest go/no-go gate.

## Challenge Every Feature

The test: for each feature, would I rather have THIS feature shipped, OR (B) 10 ambassadors, (C) 100 players, (D) 50 events listed, (E) 20 teachers, (F) 10 venues? If a pile of relationships and inventory beats the feature, the feature waits. Software is cheap to copy; the network is not.

- **Activity-first discovery (find anything mahjong near you, not just tables):** **BUILD (lightweight).** This IS the source-of-truth promise made visible, and it makes all the inventory you are gathering actually findable. But it is worthless without inventory behind it, so it ranks just under D and E: get the 50 events and 20 teachers, then this becomes the front door. Verdict: ship a simple version now, do not gold-plate it.

- **Public teacher directory:** **DELAY THE PUBLIC PAGE, CAPTURE DATA NOW.** Twenty real teachers (F's cousin) is worth far more than the page that displays them. Build the relationships and the Airtable records this month; publish the polished directory once it looks authoritative. Verdict: relationships now, public feature later.

- **Ambassador profiles:** **DELAY.** Ten activated ambassadors beat a profile system every time. Profiles are a vanity layer over the network; the network is the asset. Verdict: recruit and activate people, skip the profile UI for 30 days.

- **City pages:** **DELAY, EXCEPT ONE.** Build a single strong Las Vegas page because that is your proving ground and it showcases real density. A template of empty city pages nationally signals thinness and hurts the source-of-truth claim. Verdict: one real Las Vegas page now, national city pages after the playbook is proven.

- **Event calendar software (self-serve submission, RSVP, reminders):** **REMOVE FOR NOW.** This is the clearest A-loses case: 50 events listed (D) and 10 venues (F) crush a calendar-building project. Run events manually in Airtable. Software here is a 90-day question, not a 30-day one. Verdict: do not build.

- **Need-a-sub (sub_requests):** **REMOVE FOR NOW.** Real player pain, near-zero 30-day leverage, and it only matters once you have many active tables (which you do not yet). Handle by hand if it comes up. Verdict: do not build; do not run the SQL.

Through-line: in every case the relationships and inventory (B through F) win against new software, except the thin activity-first front door and the single Las Vegas page, which exist only to make the relationships and inventory findable. Build the network; let software follow it, not lead it.

## 30-Day Relationship Land Grab

The land grab is about controlling inventory that competitors would have to re-earn relationship by relationship. Force-ranked by 30-day leverage (impact x speed x defensibility x network effects), no ties:

**1. Teachers.** Highest leverage, and it is not close. A teacher is a node, not a leaf. One teacher carries a roster of 20 to 100 students, runs recurring open plays, knows which venues allow mahjong, and is trusted by the exact 60-to-85 audience you need. Recruit a teacher and you have effectively recruited their whole community plus inventory (their classes and open plays become listings) plus distribution (they forward your newsletter). The founder being a certified instructor makes this a peer-to-peer ask, not a cold sales pitch. This is your single best multiplier.
*First move:* Pull every instructor from the Oh My Mahjong conference relationships and the Airtable Instructors table. Founder sends a personal note, teacher to teacher: "I am building the place American Mahjong players go to find games, teachers, and events. I want you listed first and free, with a link back to your classes. Can I feature you and list your open plays?" Make a claimable teacher profile the only thing they have to say yes to.

**2. Event organizers.** Events are the most searched, most shareable, most perishable inventory, and the clearest proof of "source of truth." If you have the most complete events list, every player checks you before they check anywhere else. Organizers publish their events publicly already, so aggregation is fast and the relationship just makes it official and complete. Network effect: organizers promote their own listing to their attendees, who then discover everything else you list.
*First move:* Identify the next 90 days of known American Mahjong events from the Oh My Mahjong network and your CRM Organizations. List them now whether or not the organizer has responded (public info), then email each organizer: "You are listed. Here is your link. Send us your next three dates and we will keep you featured." Listing first, asking second, is the speed play.

**3. Tournament directors.** A specialized, prestigious subset of organizers. Fewer of them exist, which is exactly why locking them in is defensible: a near-complete tournament calendar is hard to replicate and signals authority to the whole niche. They are competitive and motivated to be found. Slightly behind general organizers only because the volume is lower, so the breadth impact is smaller.
*First move:* Same listing-first approach, framed as the "national American Mahjong tournament calendar." Being the canonical calendar is a credibility anchor for the whole site.

**4. Open play hosts.** This is your recurring-habit and local-density layer, and it overlaps heavily with teachers. High defensibility because recurring weekly plays are sticky, but lower 30-day breadth because each host covers a small geography. Worth pursuing aggressively in Las Vegas specifically, where it ties directly to table formation, and opportunistically elsewhere through teachers.

**5. Venue owners.** Strategically valuable long term (mahjong-friendly venues are a real source-of-truth category and a future revenue line), but slow in 30 days. Venue owners are businesses that need a reason to care, the sales cycle is longer, and most do not have a mahjong audience to bring you. Capture them as a byproduct of teacher and host conversations ("where do you play?"), not as a primary outreach push.

**6. Retreat operators.** High dollar value, beautiful content, but tiny in number and long lead times. A retreat is booked months out, so it does not drive 30-day momentum or network density. List them for completeness and prestige, do not spend scarce outreach hours chasing them now.

**7. Influencers.** Last, deliberately. In a niche this small with a 60-to-85 audience, generic "influencers" are the weakest lever. The real influencers ARE the teachers, organizers, and tournament directors already ranked above. Chasing social-media personalities separately is motion without inventory. See the Influencer section for how to reframe this entirely.

The through-line: ranks 1 to 3 are pure land grab (inventory plus distribution, fast, defensible). Teachers uniquely deliver players, inventory, and distribution in one yes. Start there, every day, this week.

## Ambassador Strategy

Challenge first: the current ambassador system is built around table formation, and that is too narrow for the clarified vision. Table formation is one local outcome. If ambassadors only start tables, you are building a thin local product, not a national source of truth, and you are wasting the highest-value thing an ambassador can do, which is feed the inventory machine.

But the opposite mistake is worse. Do not turn ambassadors into generic "do everything" volunteers. Volunteers do what is easy and emotionally rewarding, which is playing mahjong, not data entry. You must rank their job and make the top job nearly frictionless.

**Recommended ambassador role, ranked:**

1. **Submit events and open plays (primary).** This is the single most valuable, most leverage-rich thing an ambassador does, because it directly builds the source-of-truth inventory in their region. An ambassador in Phoenix knows the local open plays, the library group, the senior center game, the upcoming charity tournament. That is inventory you cannot get from a desk. Make this the headline job.

2. **Recruit teachers (high value, second).** Ambassadors are your local scouts for the number-one relationship type. They do not close the teacher; they identify and warm-intro them to the founder. One good teacher intro is worth more than a month of solo posting.

3. **Start and seed tables (third, and mostly Las Vegas).** Keep this, but demote it from "the whole job" to "the local habit layer where you have density." Outside Las Vegas, table formation is a natural result of having events and players listed, not a thing each ambassador must manually do.

Explicitly NOT primary: content creation and broad player recruitment. Content is nice but most of this audience is not going to produce it well or consistently, and player recruitment takes care of itself once the inventory and events exist and the newsletter distributes them. Do not make ambassadors marketers; make them scouts and inventory builders.

The tradeoff to be honest about: the more you ask an ambassador to do, the fewer will do any of it. So the design rule is one easy primary action (submit an event or open play, two-minute form), one high-value occasional action (flag a teacher to the founder), and one local-density action where it applies (seed a table). Rank it, name it, reward the top one.

**What a great ambassador does each week:**
- Submits 2 to 3 events or open plays from their area (the core habit).
- Names one teacher, host, or venue the founder should contact.
- Shares that week's newsletter with their local group or one Facebook mahjong group.
- In Las Vegas only: helps fill or seed one table.

Reward and rank publicly: the ambassador who submits the most verified inventory and the best teacher intro this month gets recognition, founder access, and early "Founding Ambassador" status. Tie the badge to inventory contribution, not to tables started. That single change realigns the whole program with the source-of-truth vision.

## Influencer Strategy (Tier A / B / C)

Reframe before tiering: in American Mahjong, "influencer" in the Instagram sense is a weak and crowded lever for a 60-to-85 audience. Influence in this niche is held by people who control communities, classes, and events. So Tiers A and B are not influencers at all. They are partners and ambassadors. Only Tier C resembles what people usually mean by "influencer," and even there the bar is a real mahjong audience, not vanity follower counts. Naming this honestly keeps you from wasting 30 days chasing reach that does not convert.

**Tier A: Partners (community leaders, head teachers, major organizers, tournament directors).**
Who: the founder's peer-level relationships from Oh My Mahjong, the most established teachers with large rosters, the people who run the marquee tournaments and the biggest regional communities. These people can move hundreds of the right players with one mention.
Offer: not a transaction, a partnership. Featured placement, a claimable profile, their events listed first and free, a permanent backlink to their business, co-promotion through the newsletter, and a genuine voice in what the platform becomes. You are offering them more reach and a stake in the category's home.
30-day move: founder makes the ask personally, peer to peer. Goal is a small number of named, locked-in anchor partners whose logos and listings make the site instantly credible. Get five of these and the whole niche treats you as the source of truth.

**Tier B: Ambassadors and active hosts (open play hosts, local organizers, mid-size teachers, engaged community members).**
Who: the people who run a weekly game, a local Facebook group, a single class, a small recurring event. They do not have national reach, but they own local density and trust.
Offer: the Founding Ambassador role, recognition, early access, founder access, and a claimable host or teacher profile. The deal is reciprocity: you list and promote their game, they feed you inventory and share the newsletter.
30-day move: convert this tier directly into the ambassador program above. They are your inventory engine. Recruit through Tier A introductions and the Las Vegas list.

**Tier C: Amplifiers (mahjong content creators and social personalities with a real, relevant audience).**
Who: people who actually post mahjong content to an audience that overlaps yours. The bar is a genuine American Mahjong following, not follower count.
Offer: lightweight and low-cost. Early access, a featured shout, co-created content around a specific event or the newsletter launch, an affiliate or referral hook so any reach is attributable.
30-day move: a handful of warm, no-cost asks to seed the newsletter and announce the launch. Cap the time you spend here. If a Tier C contact turns out to run a class, host a game, or organize an event, promote them to Tier B or A immediately, because that is where their real value is.

Bottom line: spend your scarce 30 days top-heavy. Tier A is where the source-of-truth credibility and the largest audiences are won, Tier B is where the inventory and habit are built, and Tier C is a small, cheap amplification layer you do not let distract you from the relationships that actually compound.

## Event Land Grab

**The question to challenge: are events really the largest opportunity, or just the most visible one?**

The honest case against events as #1: events are episodic, not habitual. A tournament happens once. A retreat happens once a year. A cruise is a once-a-lifetime purchase for most of this audience. If Find My Mahj Game becomes the best list of mahjong events in America, you have built a calendar that people check a few times a year. Calendars do not compound. The thing that compounds is the weekly game four women drive to every Tuesday, because that is a habit with switching costs and social lock-in. Recurring tables are the deepest moat you will ever build, and an event directory does nothing to create them. There is also a real failure mode: a national directory of everything, thin in every city, trusted in none. A list of 300 events nobody can vouch for is worse than 12 events you personally stand behind, especially with a 60-to-85 audience for whom trust is the entire product.

**The honest case for events as the land grab:** American Mahjong is a small, defined niche. In a small niche, being FIRST and MOST COMPREHENSIVE is itself a moat, because there is not room for three event directories and the first credible one becomes the default. Events, teachers, and venues are *public and aggregatable today* through relationships you already have (Oh My Mahjong network, the Las Vegas list, the Airtable CRM of instructors and organizations). You do not need to build software or wait for users to generate this inventory. A founder working the phone can capture it in 30 days. And critically, inventory is what makes someone say "check Find My Mahj Game" for the first time. Habit is what makes them say it the tenth time. You cannot get to the tenth visit without the first.

**Verdict:** Events, teachers, and venues are the fastest defensible land grab and the clearest, cheapest path to "source of truth" in the next 30 days. They earn the #1 *speed* and *breadth* priority. But inventory alone is a directory, and directories lose. The inventory only becomes a business when it is wired to two things: the **newsletter** (which converts a one-time lookup into a weekly habit and gives you a distribution channel you own) and **Las Vegas recurring tables** (which convert habit into depth and switching costs). So the ranking is not "events vs. tables." It is: capture inventory broadly because it is fast and defensible, pipe it through a newsletter because that is the habit loop, and seed Las Vegas tables because that is the depth moat. Run all three in parallel for 30 days. If forced to cut, never cut the newsletter, because without it the inventory is a museum nobody returns to.

## 30-Day Event Inventory Blitz

The goal: by day 30, Find My Mahj Game is credibly the largest single collection of American Mahjong events, teachers, and venues online. Not the prettiest. The largest and the most trusted. This is a relationship and phone-and-spreadsheet job, not a build.

**30-day numeric goals (ambitious, achievable by one founder working relationships and public sources):**

- **Events (open plays, club games, recurring sessions, one-offs): 150** listed
- **Teachers / certified instructors: 60** profiled, with at least 20 who have personally confirmed their listing
- **Venues (mahjong-friendly: clubs, senior centers, libraries, cafes, JCCs, retirement communities, game stores): 75**
- **Tournaments: 25** (the national tournament calendar is finite and public; near-complete capture is realistic)
- **Retreats: 12**
- **Cruises: 6**
- **Cities with at least one real, verified listing: 30**

These are floors, not ceilings. Tournaments, retreats, and cruises are finite enough that you should aim for *near-total* national coverage, which is exactly what makes the "source of truth" claim defensible. A competitor can copy a pretty website in a weekend. They cannot copy the fact that you already called every retreat organizer.

**How inventory is collected (manual first, no big build):**

1. **Start from what you own.** Export the Airtable CRM (Instructors, Organizations, Corporate Leads, Sessions). That is the seed list for teachers and venues. The Las Vegas email list is the seed for the first city's events. Day 1 should produce 40-plus listings before a single new call is made.
2. **Mine public sources.** National Mah Jongg League sanctioned tournament listings, Oh My Mahjong's event promotion, public Facebook groups (there are dozens of regional American Mahjong groups), Eventbrite and Meetup searches, senior center and JCC and library program calendars, mahjong retreat and cruise operators' own sites. One founder with a spreadsheet can clear most of this in two focused weeks.
3. **Work the warm network.** This is the unfair advantage. Email and call the Oh My Mahjong teachers, organizers, and studio owners: "I'm building the one place American Mahjong players go to find games and events. Send me yours and I'll list it free." Warm relationships convert at a far higher rate than cold scraping, and every organizer you list becomes a distribution node who tells their players to "check Find My Mahj Game."
4. **Open a dead-simple submission path.** One email address (events@findmymahjgame.com) and/or a basic form with five fields: what, where, when, who runs it, contact for verification. No new build. The submission box matters less as an intake tool and more as a *signal*: it tells visitors this is the place to be listed, which pulls inventory toward you.

**How it is kept honest and safe (non-negotiable for a 60-to-85 audience):**

- **Every listing gets a verification status.** "Verified" means the founder or an ambassador confirmed it with the organizer. "Public listing" means aggregated from a public source but not yet confirmed. Show the difference. Honesty about what is confirmed is itself a trust moat.
- **No private homes or shared personal contact info in public listings.** Stay consistent with the existing safety system: public meetups, venue-based, no personal addresses or phone numbers exposed. Route contact through the platform or the organizer's official channel.
- **Stale listings expire.** Anything past its date drops off; recurring listings get a quarterly re-confirm. A directory full of dead events destroys the source-of-truth claim faster than anything.
- **American Mahjong only, and stated correctly.** This is the American game (with Jokers, which are unique to American Mahjong; East is the dealer who starts). Do not dilute the directory with Chinese or Riichi events. Niche credibility is the whole point.

## Newsletter Strategy

The newsletter is not a marketing afterthought. It is the **distribution layer for the event inventory** and the **habit loop** that turns a one-time directory lookup into a weekly return visit. The inventory answers "is there anything for me?" The newsletter answers it again every week without the player having to remember to look. That is how a directory becomes a habit, and habit is the part competitors cannot copy.

**The four newsletters:** Las Vegas Mahjong Weekly, Scottsdale Mahjong Weekly, Boca Mahjong Weekly, and National Mahjong Weekly.

**Launch order: city-first, Las Vegas immediately, national later as a digest.** This is the right sequence and worth defending. A national newsletter on day 1 would be thin in every city and relevant to no one in particular; "there's a tournament 1,400 miles away" is not a reason to open an email. A *city* newsletter is dense with things a reader can actually attend this week, which is what drives opens, and opens are the habit. Start where you already have an audience and the most inventory:

1. **Las Vegas Mahjong Weekly ,  launch now.** You have the list and the home-court inventory. This is the proof of concept and the template. Do not wait for it to be perfect; send week one this week.
2. **Scottsdale Mahjong Weekly, then Boca Mahjong Weekly ,  launch once each city has roughly 8 to 10 real events to fill an issue.** Both are dense, affluent, senior-heavy American Mahjong markets, which is exactly your audience. Sequence them behind whichever hits inventory density first. Use ambassadors and the Oh My Mahjong network in each city to seed both the inventory and the initial subscriber list.
3. **National Mahjong Weekly ,  launch last, as a monthly-then-weekly digest.** Its job is different: not "what's near you this week" but "the big stuff" ,  tournaments, retreats, cruises, card-release news, the national calendar. It rolls up the finite, high-value national inventory and serves the players who travel for mahjong. It earns its place only once the inventory blitz has given it real breadth.

**Content structure (same skeleton for every issue, so it is fast to produce and familiar to readers):**

1. **This week's games and open plays** near you (the core utility, pulled straight from the inventory).
2. **One featured place or teacher** (rotates your teacher/venue inventory into the spotlight, and gives organizers a reason to want to be listed).
3. **Upcoming events** ,  tournaments, retreats, anything bigger on the horizon.
4. **Looking for a game?** A direct push back to "I Want to Play / Start a Table" ,  the table-formation feature lives inside the newsletter, not separate from it.
5. **One human note** ,  a short, warm founder voice item: a player who found a game, a new ambassador, a safety reminder. This is what makes a 75-year-old reader trust and forward it.

**Growth strategy:**

- Every event listing and every organizer you onboard becomes a subscription funnel: "Get [City] Mahjong Weekly" on every listing page and in every organizer conversation.
- Ambassadors own their city's subscriber growth; tie it to the referral attribution already built.
- Forwarding is the growth engine for this demographic. Make every issue trivially forwardable and explicitly ask: "Know someone looking for a game? Forward this." Senior players forward to their groups, and that is your cheapest, most trusted acquisition channel.
- Keep it senior-friendly and non-negotiable on accessibility: large type, high contrast, one clear column, real text not just images, plain language, obvious links.

**The loop, stated plainly:** inventory makes the first visit happen, the newsletter makes the weekly visit happen, the table-formation feature and Las Vegas recurring tables turn the visit into a habit with switching costs. Inventory is the land grab, the newsletter is the moat-in-motion, and recurring tables are the moat that lasts. Do all three in the next 30 days, and never, under any cut, drop the newsletter.

## If I Were Shauna

If I had only 30 days to make Find My Mahj Game the national source of truth for American Mahjong, here is exactly how I would live.

### My daily routine (about 3 to 4 focused hours)

I would treat this like a campaign, not a project. Here is my real day.

**Morning, first 30 minutes: Newsletter and inventory triage.**
I open my inbox and the CRM first. Anything new that came in overnight (a teacher reply, an ambassador sending an event, a player asking a question) gets logged and acted on before I do anything else. Replies to warm people go out within 24 hours, always. Slow replies kill relationships. Fast replies build them.

**Next 60 to 90 minutes: Inventory land grab. This is the non-negotiable block.**
Every single day I add real entries to our list: events, open plays, tournaments, retreats, teachers, venues. I would set a hard daily number and hit it no matter what. Say 15 to 25 new verified entries a day. Public Facebook groups, senior center calendars, library event pages, JCC and country club calendars, the Oh My Mahjong network, tournament listings, cruise and retreat operators. I do not wait for it to be pretty. A messy but complete list beats a beautiful empty one. If I do nothing else on a given day, I do this.

**Next 45 to 60 minutes: Relationships. Teachers and Ambassadors.**
I would personally reach out to 5 teachers and 3 potential ambassadors a day. Not a blast. Real, warm, one-to-one messages, ideally referencing someone we both know from the conference. The ask is simple: "We are building the one place players go to find all the mahjong. Can I list your classes and events, and would you send me the ones I am missing?" Teachers say yes to free promotion. Ambassadors say yes to being recognized as their city's champion. I track every conversation in the CRM so nothing falls through.

**Last 30 minutes: Las Vegas tables and the newsletter draft.**
A little every day on the home base. Confirm a recurring table, fill an empty seat, nudge a group toward a standing weekly time. And I keep a running newsletter draft open all week, dropping in the best event, the newest teacher, the most interesting find as I go, so that by send day it writes itself.

**Once a week, a fixed day, the newsletter goes out. No exceptions.**
Same day, same time, every week, starting this week. Even a thin first issue beats a perfect one that slips. The habit of sending is more important than the contents of any single issue. This is how "check Find My Mahj Game" becomes a reflex.

### What I would aggressively pursue

- **Inventory, inventory, inventory.** This is the land grab and the moat. Being first and most complete in a small niche is the win. I would be slightly obsessed with the number going up every day.
- **The Oh My Mahjong relationships.** This is our unfair advantage and it has a shelf life. Warm contacts go cold. I would work that network hard and fast while it is still warm, before a competitor thinks to.

- **The newsletter as the habit layer.** Inventory with no distribution is a filing cabinet. The newsletter is how the inventory reaches people every week and turns into a habit.

### What I would ignore

- **Vanity polish.** Pixel-perfect pages, logo tweaks, color debates. None of it adds a single event or relationship in 30 days.

- **Metrics that do not matter.** Page views, time on site, follower counts. The only numbers I care about are inventory added, teachers and ambassadors engaged, newsletter sent and opened, and Las Vegas tables actually playing.

### What I would stop building

- **No new software for 30 days unless it directly removes a bottleneck to gathering inventory or sending the newsletter.** The platform already does enough. Every hour spent on a new feature is an hour not spent gathering the inventory or working a relationship that is the actual moat.

If a simple spreadsheet or the existing Airtable lets me start gathering and tracking today, I start today and let the database catch up later. The work cannot wait on the plumbing.
- **No big redesigns, no rebuilds, no "let me first set up the perfect system."** The perfect system that ships on Day 25 loses to the messy system that captured the market by Day 10.

### The one sentence I would live by

In a niche this small, the winner is not whoever has the best software. It is whoever gathers the most, knows the most people, and gets there first. So I would spend almost every working hour on exactly those three things, and let everything else wait.
