# City Launch Plan: Scottsdale / Phoenix

Second proof market. Deep mahjong and active-adult roots, strong teacher presence. One honest caveat shapes this whole plan: we are launching in summer. Phoenix and Scottsdale are major snowbird markets, so a large share of seasonal residents are away from roughly late spring through early fall, and the heat is extreme. We treat this launch as a base-building launch: prove that an ambassador can form and fill real tables with year-round residents now, and prime the market for the fall and winter surge.

## Targets (30 days)

- Ambassador target: 2 (ideally one teacher and one community organizer), stretch to 3.
- Table target: 6 tables created, 3 filled, 1 recurring.
- Players reached: 15 or more distinct year-round residents.
- Status goal: reach Green by Day 30, where Green in summer means at least 1 recurring table plus a built base (teachers and venues lined up) for fall.

## Why these targets are lower than Las Vegas

This is not a weaker market, it is an off-season month. Setting Las Vegas-level table targets here would push the ambassador to chase players who are out of town and would teach us the wrong lesson. We measure success in summer by a real recurring table plus a primed pipeline, then we expect the numbers to climb sharply in October and November.

## 30-day launch checklist

### Week 0: Setup
- [ ] Confirm the lead ambassador (a year-round teacher is ideal).
- [ ] Assign referral code, for example FMM-PHX-{FIRSTNAME}.
- [ ] List 3 indoor, air-conditioned venue candidates (library, JCC, community center, senior center).
- [ ] Ask the ambassador to identify their year-round players specifically, not seasonal ones.

### Week 1: First table with year-round players
- [ ] Lead ambassador starts their first table on /start.
- [ ] Invite year-round students and players to claim seats.
- [ ] Begin a teacher-and-venue base list (every teacher and venue that says "yes, in the fall" goes on it).
- [ ] Milestone: 1 table created and filling.

### Week 2: Fill one and build the base
- [ ] Fill the first table (4 of 4).
- [ ] Confirm 1 indoor venue partner.
- [ ] Sign a second ambassador (organizer or a second teacher).
- [ ] Add at least 3 names to the fall base list (teachers or venues).
- [ ] Milestone: 2 to 3 tables created, 1 filled, first game played.

### Week 3: Recurring and beginner pipeline
- [ ] Turn the filled table into a recurring game.
- [ ] Run a small beginner-friendly session at the indoor venue.
- [ ] Milestone: 4 tables created, 2 filled, 1 recurring.

### Week 4: Lock the base for fall
- [ ] Confirm the recurring game is holding.
- [ ] Finalize the fall surge plan: which venues and teachers will host when snowbirds return, and a waitlist of seasonal players to contact in October.
- [ ] Document what worked with year-round players.
- [ ] Milestone: 6 tables created, 3 filled, 1 recurring, plus a ready fall pipeline. Status Green.

## Risks

1. Snowbird departure. A large share of the most active players are out of town in summer. Chasing them wastes the month.
2. Extreme heat. Any non-indoor plan fails. Heat also keeps some residents home and less social.
3. Smaller summer player pool. Tables may fill slower than in peak season.
4. Ambassador also travels. Teachers and organizers may be away part of the summer too.

## Recovery actions

1. For snowbird departure: explicitly target year-round residents now, and build a named waitlist of seasonal players to activate in October. Do not count seasonal players who are away toward the summer targets.
2. For heat: indoor, air-conditioned venues only, set as the default. Schedule games for mornings.
3. For a thin summer pool: if filling lags, concentrate on one excellent recurring table rather than many half-full ones. One sticky group is the proof we need.
4. For an absent ambassador: sign two ambassadors so coverage holds when one travels, and keep the base list moving even in a quiet week.
