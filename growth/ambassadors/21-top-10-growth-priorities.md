# Top 10 Growth Priorities (with a hard look at the plan)

This document does the thing that was asked: it challenges the plan rather than validating it, then ranks the ten highest-ROI actions available right now. ROI here means recurring tables per unit of founder effort, not traffic.

## Where the plan is weak or blind

1. The biggest blind spot is an asset we already own and barely use. Las Vegas Mahjong has a real email list (the full mahjong email list in Drive) and a live CRM in Airtable with People, Sessions, and Attendance, real local students and players. That is a pool of warm, local, ready players in our proof market. The fastest path to the first real tables is not recruiting new ambassadors or chasing influencers; it is converting existing Las Vegas students into the first FindMyMahj tables this week. The plan reaches outward when the highest-ROI move is sitting in our own database.

2. "Tables formed" is a gameable metric. Several proposed rewards key off the count of tables created. A table created and never filled is worthless, and rewarding creation invites padding. Reward filled, played, and recurring only. The plan should stop treating "formed" as the prize; it is just the first step.

3. The influencer strategy overreaches. Tiers B and C produce traffic, and the stated goal is not traffic. Tier A is just ambassadors by another name. Splitting effort across three influencer tiers dilutes the one motion that works (local leaders). The honest version of "influencer strategy" is "recruit more local leaders."

4. Launch grants risk attracting the wrong people. Cash for signing up draws perk-seekers and creates admin overhead that does not scale to a national volunteer network. Money should only ever reimburse real, post-activation costs (a venue, refreshments), capped. "Marketing credits" are undefined because we have no paid product to credit.

5. We are stronger on the connector than the supply, but supply is the real constraint. The hard part is not awareness; it is getting four specific people to commit to a standing time. The plan invests in attention (influencers, awareness) when the binding constraint is committed players and a reliable place to meet. Venues and the want-to-play pool deserve more weight than influencer reach.

6. Founder dependence is an unmanaged risk. If the founder seeds every table, we have proven the founder, not the playbook. The plan needs a deliberate step-back and a separate measure of ambassador-driven tables, or we will scale the founder's calendar, not the business.

7. Some promised ambassador benefits cannot be delivered yet. Featured city profiles and teacher listings depend on pages that are not live. Promising them now is a check we cannot cash; sequence them to when those pages ship.

8. Timing is underweighted. Two of the three launch cities are snowbird markets in their off-season right now. Holding them to the same targets as Las Vegas will read as failure when it is just seasonality. Judge them on base-building.

9. We have not defined a kill rule. There is no written line for when a dormant ambassador or a stalled city gets cut. Without it, founder time leaks into hopeless cases. Decide the pivot and the cut in advance.

10. An opportunity we are missing: the "need a 4th" moment is the most viral, highest-intent event in the whole product, and it is underused as a growth loop. Every host short a player is a warm invitation to pull in a new player. Lean into it hard (priority referrals, the want-to-play pool, and later Need a Sub) as the core acquisition mechanic, rather than top-of-funnel awareness.

## The 10 highest-ROI actions, ranked

Ranked by recurring tables produced per unit of founder effort.

1. Mine the existing Las Vegas assets now. Pull the lasvegasmahj.com email list and the Airtable People/Attendance records, and personally convert existing local students and players into the first 5 to 10 real FindMyMahj tables this week. Warm, local, fast, and it proves the loop in the proof market. Highest ROI by far.

2. Sign and activate 3 Las Vegas ambassadors from those same relationships. Get each to one filled, recurring table. This is the proof that an ambassador, not the founder, can run it.

3. Work the Oh My Mahjong Hot list in the three proof and launch cities. Personally recruit and activate 6 to 8 local leaders, starting their first table on the call. Use the new Warm Relationship Pipeline to track and never let a signed contact go dormant.

4. Turn every filled table into a recurring game. Make the recurring ask immediate and explicit after the first game. Recurring tables are the entire North Star and the deepest moat; this costs almost nothing and compounds.

5. Lock one public venue per proof city (library, community center, or senior center). Removes the single biggest practical barrier (where do we meet) and anchors repeat play. Supply, not awareness, is the constraint.

6. Use the now-live attribution and heat map every week. Let the heat map tell you which cities have demand and no tables, and place the next ambassador there. Let attribution show which ambassadors actually produce, and concentrate on them. Decisions over guesses.

7. Recruit Tier A local leaders only (teachers, studio owners, club and Facebook group admins). Skip Tier B and C for now. These are the only "influencers" who convert attention into seated tables.

8. Adopt the activation-first reward structure. Status and access at signup; priority referrals, venue sponsorship, and reimbursements only after a filled, played table. Stop rewarding signups and "formed" counts.

9. Get the real teacher directory to 20 published teachers, entered by ambassadors through the new admin workflow. This unlocks the teacher directory and is a prerequisite for real city pages, and teachers bring students who become players.

10. Run a weekly founder review on the City Launch Scorecard and Warm Relationship Pipeline, with a written kill rule. Double down on producers, re-engage or release the dormant, and protect founder time for the actions above.

## What to stop or defer

- Defer Tier B and C influencer outreach until the proof cities work.
- Defer broad launch grants; reimburse post-activation costs only.
- Defer lifestyle and national awareness pushes; they create traffic, not tables.
- Do not reward "tables formed" counts; reward filled, played, recurring.

## The one-sentence strategy

Convert the relationships and players we already have into recurring local games, prove it in three cities with attribution and a venue in each, and reward only the people who produce real games, before a funded competitor can build the trust and habit that money cannot rush.
