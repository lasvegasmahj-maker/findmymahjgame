# National Mahjong Hub Strategy

The question on the table: should Find My Mahj Game evolve from "find people to play" into "the national destination for American Mahjong." This document evaluates that honestly, challenges it, and gives a verdict.

## The verdict, up front

The destination vision is probably the right long-term north. It is the wrong thing to build right now, and building it the way it is framed (multiple teams, a national calendar, a hub of everything) would likely kill the momentum we have. The defensible path is the opposite of "go national wide": win local density in a few cities, where habit and trust actually form, and let the national hub emerge as the sum of won cities. Earn the right to expand.

The one near-term exception worth pulling forward is a weekly local newsletter, because it is the cheapest tool that makes players return, it can run manually this week with the existing Las Vegas email list, and it requires zero engineering. That is the wartime insight: the highest-leverage "hub" feature is also the most manual and the fastest.

## Why "national destination" is dangerous right now

- A national directory of everything (events, teachers, businesses, calendars) is broad and shallow. Broad and shallow is exactly what a funded competitor out-builds, and exactly what dilutes the one metric that matters now: recurring tables.
- The return-weekly problem is not solved by more listings. It is solved by your own standing game and a weekly local reason to check in. Listings without local density are empty pages.
- We have not yet proven the core loop even in one city. Spinning up five teams to build a hub before a single market is won is premature scaling, the most common way ambitious startups die.
- The brand and the wedge ("find a game") are player-discovery framed. The hub vision may eventually need broader positioning, but rebranding before the wedge is proven is a distraction.

## Why the vision is still right long-term

- American Mahjong is a real, passionate, underserved national community with no clear digital home. That vacuum is a genuine opportunity.
- The pieces compound. Players bring hosts, hosts bring tables, teachers bring students, open plays and a local newsletter bring everyone back weekly, businesses want access to the gathered audience. Done in the right order, these reinforce each other.
- The moat (local trust, recurring habit, density) is exactly what a well-funded competitor cannot buy quickly. If we build it city by city, we own something durable.

So: yes to the destination as the horizon, no to building it now. Build the wedge, then the local hub, then the national hub.

## The smallest possible "hub" that earns weekly visits

Strip the grand vision to the minimum that makes a player check weekly:
1. My game: the status of my own recurring table and any "need a 4th" near me. (The strongest habit is the game itself.)
2. New tables and open plays near me this week. (A reason to look that changes weekly.)
3. A weekly local note: what is happening in mahjong in my city. (The newsletter, manual at first.)

That is it. Three things, all local, all tied to real activity. Everything else (tournaments, leagues, cruises, retreats, a national calendar, a business directory) is a Phase 2 or 3 layer that only has value once a city is dense.

## Ranking the ten opportunities

Scored on user value, speed to launch, network effects, defensibility, and revenue. Ranked by what to do first.

| Opportunity | User value | Speed | Network effects | Defensibility | Revenue | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Player discovery (core loop) | High | Live | High | High | Low now | Phase 1, the foundation |
| Local newsletter | High | Very fast (manual) | Medium | Medium | Medium later | Pull forward, manual now |
| Open plays | High | Fast (manual listings) | High | Medium | Low | Phase 2 |
| Teachers | Medium-high | Medium | Medium | Medium | Medium | Phase 2 |
| Local ambassadors | High | Live | High | High | Low | Phase 1-2, the engine |
| Mahjong-friendly businesses | Medium | Slow | Medium | Medium | High | Phase 2-3, the revenue layer |
| Event calendar | Medium | Medium | Medium | Low-medium | Low | Phase 2, not the center |
| Tournaments | Medium (niche) | Slow | Low (episodic) | Low | Medium | Phase 3, listings/partnership |
| Leagues | High (recurring) | Slow (ops heavy) | Medium | Medium | Medium | Phase 3, emerges from recurring tables |
| Cruises and retreats | Low (infrequent) | Slow | Low | Low | Medium (affiliate) | Later, partnership only |

Pattern: the things that reinforce the local weekly loop (discovery, newsletter, open plays, teachers, ambassadors) come first. The episodic, travel, and monetization layers (tournaments, leagues, cruises, retreats, businesses) wait, and most should be listings or partnerships rather than builds.

## The phased roadmap

Phase 1: Prove player discovery (now, next 90 days). Recurring tables in Las Vegas via existing assets and ambassadors. Success = the first 5 recurring tables and a rising organic ratio. Nothing else gets built.

Phase 2: Become local mahjong discovery (after Phase 1, per city). In a city that is already dense, add the local newsletter (already running manually), open plays, the teacher directory, and the first mahjong-friendly venues. Success = in one city, a player can find a game, a teacher, and an open play, all real.

Phase 3: Become the national mahjong hub (later). Replicate local hubs across cities. Add tournaments, leagues, retreats, and cruises as listings and partnerships. Monetize through the business program. Success = the default national destination, built from many strong local hubs, not a thin national directory.

## The honest bottom line

The national hub is a fine destination and a terrible next step. The fastest path to becoming the place every American mahjong player checks regularly is not to build a hub; it is to make a few cities so locally alive (recurring games, a weekly local note, real teachers and open plays) that being on the platform becomes a habit, then repeat. Resist the urge to build wide before we are deep. The separate strategy docs (calendar, newsletter, teachers, businesses, return-visit, and the 90-day wartime plan) each apply this same test: does it make a real local player return this week, and can we do it manually first.
