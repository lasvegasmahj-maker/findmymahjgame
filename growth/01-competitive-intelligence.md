## (2) The 5–7 Biggest WHITE-SPACE Opportunities

1. **"Find a 4th / find individual players near me" — TRULY UNOWNED.** Bam Good Time (the strongest competitor) confirms on their own site they have *no individual player directory*. The only thing serving this intent is Sloperama's 1990s bulletin board and ephemeral Facebook posts. This is the single biggest gap and it maps exactly to your `player_listings` table. **Nobody owns it. You can.**

2. **SEO-indexed, persistent versions of Facebook-group demand.** 103k+ people in one FB group post "looking for a game in [neighborhood]" and those posts evaporate, invisible to Google. A persistent, location-indexed, Google-crawlable page for *every* city's players/games captures the same intent that's currently locked inside Meta's walled garden.

3. **The "near me" long-tail at city + neighborhood granularity.** Incumbents stop at state and ~50 top cities (Bam) or thin city pages (MahjongCompare). Programmatic, genuinely-useful pages for the next 500–2,000 cities/suburbs (the Naples, Winston-Salems, Scottsdales) is wide open. Your 50 state pages + city seed data is the skeleton — fill it with real listings.

4. **JCC / library / senior-center / community-center inventory aggregation.** This is the real backbone of in-person play and it's almost entirely un-indexed (buried in PDFs and bulletin boards). Aggregating it is grunt work no product brand will do — and it's defensible because it's a data moat, not a feature.

5. **A neutral, cross-brand "open play & drop-in game" layer.** Every brand only lists *its own* events. No one aggregates open-play sessions across all clubs/instructors/venues into one "what can I drop into this week near me" view. That's the highest-frequency, highest-retention use case (weekly, not once).

6. **Seasonal NMJL card-cycle capture.** Every spring the card releases and a wave of new/returning players search. NMJL owns the card but offers zero discovery. A "new card just dropped — find a game/teacher near you to learn the new hands" play is a predictable annual traffic spike no one is engineered to catch.

7. **Player-to-player matching as a network effect (not a static list).** Directories are static. The moment you let a solo player say "I'm in [zip], beginner, weekday mornings" and surface matches/games, you create a two-sided network that compounds — the more players list, the more valuable it is, and it's the one thing a product brand (Oh My Mahjong) or a SaaS (Bam) won't build because it doesn't sell tiles or club seats.

## (3) The Single Sharpest Wedge to Win First

**Own "find players / find a game near me" for the individual player — the intent Sloperama proves exists, Facebook hosts but hides, and Bam Good Time explicitly does not serve.**

Concretely, for this sprint (aligned to your KPIs: USERS, LISTINGS, CITY COVERAGE):

- **Lead with player listings, not clubs.** Bam already has 1,218 clubs and a SaaS moat there — do NOT fight them on club management. Compete where they're empty: solo players looking for a 4th. Your free `player_listings` flow (`/list-my-game`) IS the product. Reframe it from "list your game" to **"Find a 4th near you"** / "Find players near you" — that's the search intent.
- **Beat Sloperama on day one.** It's a manually-emailed, Ctrl-F bulletin board. A searchable, filterable (level/day/format), location-indexed, instantly-published listing wins immediately and inherits the demand it proves.
- **Harvest Facebook demand into Google.** Every "looking for a game in [neighborhood]" FB post is a listing you should own as a permanent, crawlable page. Seed coverage city-by-city, then let the spring card cycle and FB cross-posting drive listings.
- **Wedge → moat:** start with one intent (find players) in your strongest geographies (anchor on Las Vegas via the sister site + the largest metros), get the two-sided flywheel spinning, *then* expand into open-play aggregation and JCC/venue inventory once you own the player graph nobody else has.

**Why this wins:** it's the one square on the board that the governing body, the SaaS incumbent, the lifestyle giant, and the affiliate site have all left empty — and it's a network-effect data moat, not a copyable feature.
