## 1. Executive summary

Find My Mahj Game helps people find mahjong players and get into real games. In this sprint we did two big things. First, we turned the website from a directory you browse into a tool that actually forms games: a person can find a game near them, start their own, or ask a real person for help. Second, we built a complete growth system to spread it city by city through trusted local leaders called Founding Ambassadors, many of whom you met at the Oh My Mahjong conference.

The product is live today. The growth plan is documented and ready to run. We are now in City Launch Mode, which means we stop adding features and focus all effort on one thing: getting real tables to form, fill, get played, and repeat. We will prove the approach in three cities (Las Vegas first, then Scottsdale/Phoenix and Boca Raton/Palm Beach) before expanding nationally.

The single most important idea: one ambassador leads to multiple tables, which leads to multiple players, which leads to recurring games. Recurring games are the goal, because a standing weekly game is a community that lasts.

## 2. What we built

- A simple way for players to find a game, start a game, or get help, designed for older adults with big buttons and plain words.
- An automatic coordination system that emails a group when their table is full and guides them to a safe public place to meet.
- A safety model that keeps home addresses and personal contact details private.
- A way to track whether games were actually played, not just signed up for.
- A Founding Ambassador program: an application page, an approval process, and a full kit of recruiting, onboarding, and launch materials.
- A measurement system: a one-page weekly dashboard and a city scorecard with a simple Red, Yellow, Green status.
- Three city launch plans and a 30-day plan to run the whole effort.

## 4. Product overview

The product is built around three doors, all reachable in one tap from the home screen.

- I Want to Play is for someone who wants a game. They tell us their area, we show them games to join, and they claim a seat with just a first name and a phone or email.
- Start a Table is for someone who wants to host. They set a day, time, and general area, and invite people. The site shows the seats filling up.
- I Need Help is for anyone who feels stuck. It connects them to a real person.

When a table reaches four players, the site does the coordinating automatically: it emails everyone with first names and the general area, recommends a public place for the first game, and routes replies to the host so the group can pick a spot. After they play, the host can start the same game again next week, which is how a one-time game becomes a standing one.

## 5. Safety model

Safety is built into every step, because our players are often older adults and trust is everything.

- We never publish or share home addresses or exact locations.
- We never show anyone's phone number or email to other players.
- New groups are guided to meet in public places, like libraries, community centers, cafes, or senior centers, for their first game.
- Hosts coordinate through us, so people do not have to hand out personal contact details to strangers.
- There is a clear way to report a problem.

This is also a competitive strength. A safe, trusted experience for older players is hard for a newcomer to copy quickly.

## 6. Growth strategy

We grow city by city through trusted local leaders, not through advertising. The relationships you built at the Oh My Mahjong conference are our highest-leverage asset, because trust travels through people, not ads.

The motion is one simple loop, repeated in each city: find a local leader, make a warm ask, approve them, help them start their first table within two weeks, hand them ready-made materials, and help them turn a full table into a standing weekly game. Then we measure, reward what works, and repeat in the next city.

We are deliberately not chasing revenue right now. The goal is real games and recurring tables. A strong base of recurring games is both the mission and the moat.

## 7. Ambassador strategy

Founding Ambassadors are trusted local leaders, mainly teachers, studio owners, organizers, and event hosts. One ambassador can launch a city.

We built everything an ambassador needs:
- A recruiting approach with a tailored reason to join for each type of leader.
- Five ready-to-send recruiting emails.
- A welcome sequence that gets a new ambassador to their first table within about two weeks.
- A launch kit of copy-and-paste materials: invitations, social posts, a flyer, a player FAQ, and safety talking points.
- A week-by-week city checklist.
- A plan to give each ambassador a simple referral code so we can credit the tables they create.

The national goal is 25 ambassadors in 25 cities, but we prove it in three cities first. The number we protect is activated ambassadors, meaning they actually started a real table. Signed but inactive does not count.

## 8. City launch strategy

We prove the playbook in three markets before going national.

- Las Vegas is the primary proof market. It is our home market, it is a year-round city, and you are on the ground, so a summer launch works. Thirty-day goal: 3 ambassadors, 10 tables started, 6 filled, 3 standing weekly games.
- Scottsdale and Phoenix is a strong mahjong area, but summer is off-season because many seasonal residents leave. We treat this month as base-building: prove one standing game with year-round residents and line up teachers and venues for the fall.
- Boca Raton and Palm Beach has some of the deepest mahjong roots in the country, but it is also off-season in summer and in hurricane season. Same approach: prove one or two standing games now and bank a strong teacher base for the season.

A City Launch Scorecard tracks every city with a Red, Yellow, or Green status, so you always know which city needs help this week. A city is proven when it reaches Green and holds it: an ambassador other than you has formed and filled tables, with at least one standing weekly game that keeps playing on its own.

## 9. Metrics that matter

We measure success in four steps, in this order:

1. Tables Formed. Someone started a game.
2. Tables Filled. It reached four players.
3. Games Played. The group actually met and played.
4. Recurring Tables. The group plays on a standing schedule.

Everything else is noise. Revenue is not a goal right now. Recurring tables are the prize. You review these every Friday on a one-page dashboard, and the city scorecard shows each market's status at a glance.

## 10. Top risks

- We may not have enough warm relationships. The plan assumes about 25 warm contacts across about 25 cities. Only you know the real number.
- The real Las Vegas player pool is unknown. The targets assume you can personally bring a meaningful number of players.
- Summer timing hurts the snowbird cities. Phoenix and Boca will be slower in summer, so we judge them on base-building, not table counts.
- Founder dependence. If only you can make tables happen, we have proven you, not the playbook. Success requires an ambassador to run a standing game.
- Competition. If a funded competitor copied the idea, our protection is trust: the relationships, the local ambassadors, the safety reputation, and standing games that are hard to fake.
