## 1. What we built

We turned Find My Mahj Game from a directory into a tool that actually gets people playing. The heart of it is simple: a person can find a game near them, start their own game, or ask for help, all in a few taps. Around that, we built a complete program to recruit local leaders (Founding Ambassadors) who can launch mahjong in their own cities, and a set of simple operating documents so you can run the whole effort from one page.

In short: a product that forms real tables, and a growth system to spread it city by city.

## 2. What is live today

These are working right now on the website:

- I Want to Play. A visitor types their town or uses their location and finds games near them.
- Start a Table. A host picks a day, a time, and their general area, then invites players. It takes about a minute.
- I Need Help. A friendly page to reach a real person, for anyone who feels stuck.
- Need a 4th. When a table has 3 of 4 players, it clearly shows one seat is left, which is the moment people rush to join.
- The safety model. New groups are guided to meet in public places for the first game. We never show home addresses or share anyone's phone number or email.
- Games Played tracking. After a table is full, we can confirm the group actually played, so we measure real games, not just signups.
- A Founding Ambassador page where local leaders apply, with an automatic thank-you to them and a notification to you, and a simple review screen where you approve them.
- The phone-friendly home screen, with the three main actions right at the top so a first-time visitor knows what to do in seconds.

## 3. What problems we solved

- People could browse mahjong listings but had no easy way to actually get into a game. Now they can find one, start one, or get help.
- Older players found typical apps confusing. We made big buttons, plain words, and the option to use a phone number or an email, no account required.
- Safety worried people. We removed exact locations from the process and built in public-meeting guidance and clear privacy promises.
- We had no way to know if real games happened. Now we track tables filling and games being played.
- We had no engine for growth. Now we have a repeatable way to recruit a local leader and help them launch a city.

## 4. What we learned

- The thing that matters is not signups, it is tables that fill and games that repeat. Everything is now organized around that.
- An ambassador who signs but never starts a table does not help. The number to protect is activated ambassadors, meaning they started a real table.
- Timing matters. It is summer, which is the off-season for snowbird cities like Phoenix and Boca Raton, so those launches should focus on building a base now and harvesting in the fall, while Las Vegas (a year-round city and our home market) is the best place to prove the playbook this month.
- Trust is our edge. Warm relationships and a safe, senior-friendly experience are hard for anyone to copy quickly.

## 5. Current strategy

We are in City Launch Mode. Product work is now in maintenance, meaning we only build something new if it directly increases tables formed, tables filled, games played, or recurring games. The focus is the Oh My Mahjong ambassador launch: recruit trusted local leaders, help each one launch their city, and prove the approach in three markets before going national.

The motion is one simple loop, repeated city by city: find a local leader, make a warm ask, approve them, help them start their first table within two weeks, give them ready-made materials, and help them turn a full table into a standing weekly game. Then measure, reward what works, and repeat.

## 6. The North Star metrics

We measure success in four steps, in this order:

1. Tables Formed. Someone started a game.
2. Tables Filled. The game reached four players.
3. Games Played. The group actually met and played.
4. Recurring Tables. The group plays on a standing schedule.

Revenue is not a goal right now. Recurring tables are the prize, because a standing weekly game is a community that lasts.

## 7. The 3 proof markets

We will prove the playbook in three cities first:

- Las Vegas. Our home market and a year-round city, so a summer launch works. This is the primary proof. Goal in 30 days: 3 ambassadors, 10 tables started, 6 filled, 3 standing weekly games.
- Scottsdale and Phoenix. Strong mahjong area, but summer is off-season. Treat this month as base-building: prove one standing game with year-round residents and line up teachers and venues for the fall.
- Boca Raton and Palm Beach. Some of the deepest mahjong roots in the country, but also off-season in summer and in hurricane season. Same approach: prove one or two standing games now and bank a strong teacher base for the season.

Each city has its own one-page plan with targets, a week-by-week checklist, risks, and recovery steps. A City Launch Scorecard tracks every city with a simple Red, Yellow, or Green status so you always know which city needs help this week.

## 8. The ambassador strategy

Founding Ambassadors are trusted local leaders, mainly teachers, studio owners, organizers, and event hosts, many of whom you met at the Oh My Mahjong conference. Those relationships are our single highest-leverage asset.

The idea: one ambassador leads to multiple tables, which leads to multiple players, which leads to recurring games. One person can launch a city.

We built everything an ambassador needs: a recruiting approach, ready-to-send invitation emails, a step-by-step welcome that gets them to their first table, a launch kit of copy-and-paste materials (invitations, social posts, a flyer, a player FAQ, safety talking points), and a week-by-week city checklist. We also built a plan to give each ambassador a simple referral code so we can credit the tables they create.

The 30-day goal nationally is 25 ambassadors in 25 cities, but we prove it in the three markets above first.

- Do we really have enough warm relationships? The plan assumes about 25 warm contacts across about 25 cities. Only you know the real number and which cities they cover.
- How many players can you personally bring in Las Vegas right now? The Las Vegas targets assume a real local pool. We need your honest number.

- Founder dependence. If only you can make tables happen, we have proven you, not the playbook. Success means an ambassador, not you, runs a standing game.
- Competition. If a funded competitor copied the idea, our protection is trust: the relationships, the local ambassadors, the safety reputation, and standing weekly games that are hard to fake.
