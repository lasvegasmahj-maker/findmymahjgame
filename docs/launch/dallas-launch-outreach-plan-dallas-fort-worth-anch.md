## Why anchors first

DFW is the liquidity-proof market. The whole Dallas Test rides on real tables forming and filling in this metro, so the fastest path is to win the people who already gather players, not to find players one by one. Five named groups already pull DFW mahjong players together. If even two of them say yes, you inherit a warm room instead of building one cold.

These five are TARGETS, not confirmed partners. Before you send a word, verify each one: is it a private home group, a teacher's paid class, a Facebook group, or a venue-based open play? Who actually runs it, and what is their first name and best channel (email, DM, or a mutual contact)? The messages below are written to work no matter which it turns out to be, but a true first name and one real detail beats any template. Never claim a count, a roster size, or a quote you cannot source.

## What you are offering them (say it the same way every time)

- A free national service whose one job is helping mahjong people find their people and form real games.
- Money never crosses the table. No fees to players, no fees to you, no booking cuts, no pay-for-placement, ever.
- You do the part you are already good at (gathering players). The site does the coordinating (seat counter, the automatic "set up your first game" note when four are in).
- Contact info is never shared between players. First names only in anything public. No home addresses, ever. New groups meet in a public place for the first game.
- For organizers and teachers, every table started through your link is credited to you.

## The honest line about the matcher and the Bench

Dallas is the only market where the stranger matcher and the Bench (our pool of subs and fourth-chair players) run at launch, founder-approved, capped while we learn. That is a feature for these anchors, not a footnote: it means a club here can fill a missing fourth from the Bench instead of canceling a game. Mention it only to groups that lose games to no-shows. Do not oversell it. It is a pilot.

## The target list (framing for each anchor)

For each, the column "Most likely type" is your working hypothesis to verify, not a fact. "The fit" is why they matter. "The ask" is the single yes you want from the first message.

### 1. Charleston Club

- Most likely type: an established, name-recognized DFW group or club. Treat as your highest-value anchor until you learn otherwise.
- The fit: an established club has the exact thing a new market lacks, a room of players who already show up. One yes here can seed multiple tables in week one of Welcome Week.
- The ask: a 15-minute call to show how their members can use FindMyMahj to fill open seats and find subs, at no cost.
- First message: see Message A below.

### 2. Dallas Mahj Club

- Most likely type: a Dallas-area club or organizer, possibly Facebook-based.
- The fit: a club that already carries the city's name in its own name is a natural partner and, frankly, a natural Founding Ambassador. They benefit most from the credited link.
- The ask: invite the organizer to be a Founding Ambassador for Dallas, or at minimum to let members list open seats.
- First message: see Message B below.

### 3. Peace Love Mahjong

- Most likely type: a teacher, a small studio, or a community brand (the name reads like a teacher or boutique brand more than a venue).
- The fit: if it is a teacher, this is your Dallas Mentor track. Per our Oh My Mahjong approach, Founding Teacher invites go to Dallas Mentors first. A teacher gives you new players after every class who need a real game to keep playing in.
- The ask: a short call to talk about the Founding Teacher invite and how graduates can find a standing game.
- First message: see Message C below.

### 4. Mahj Clubhouse

- Most likely type: a venue-style or "clubhouse" open-play space, possibly a recurring location.
- The fit: a clubhouse is both a gathering of players and a possible standing venue. Two assets in one. If it is a physical space, it can host listed games; if it is a group name, it is another club partner.
- The ask: a call to explore listing their open plays so DFW players can find them, with the Bench available to backfill a missing fourth.
- First message: see Message D below.

### 5. J Mahj Room

- Most likely type: a teacher's room, a private studio, or a small invitation group ("Room" suggests a single space or a host's home group).
- The fit: small, warm groups are where recurring tables come from. If it is a host with a tight group, the Bench solves their real pain (someone drops, the game dies). If it is a teacher, it joins the Dallas Mentor track.
- The ask: a low-key call. Lead with the sub-finding pain, not with a pitch.
- First message: see Message E below.

## How to run this in Welcome Week (week of July 13)

1. Verify all five before sending. Confirm what each group actually is and who runs it. Fix the type hypothesis above against reality. If one turns out not to exist or not to be active, drop it and do not invent a replacement claim.
2. Send the anchors first, on Monday and Tuesday of Welcome Week, one message each, personalized with a real detail. These five come before the broader Top 25 ambassador sequence, because one anchor yes is worth dozens of cold contacts.
3. Sequence the rest. Run the standard five-email recruiting sequence (growth/ambassadors/02-recruiting-email-sequence.md) for any individual teachers, organizers, and event hosts in DFW who are not one of these five anchors.
4. Reply same day. Book a 15-minute call the moment anyone says yes. For a yes, point them to findmymahjgame.com/ambassadors (organizers) or your Founding Teacher invite (teachers).
5. Honor the order on teachers. Teacher invites in Dallas go to your Dallas Mentors first, per the Oh My Mahjong approach. Peace Love Mahjong and any teacher behind J Mahj Room belong on that track.
6. Track every anchor in one row each: name, verified type, contact, channel, date sent, reply, outcome. Feed it into the founder calendar and the city launch scorecard.

## What a win looks like by Day 14

- At least 2 of the 5 anchors in a real conversation.
- At least 1 anchor agreeing to let members list open seats, or one organizer signing on as a Founding Ambassador, or one teacher accepting the Founding Teacher invite.
- First DFW tables listed and beginning to fill, with the Bench available to backfill a fourth.

That is the start of the liquidity the Dallas Test measures. Anchors are the shortest path to it.
