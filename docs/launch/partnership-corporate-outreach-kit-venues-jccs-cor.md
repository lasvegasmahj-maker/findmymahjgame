## Part 1: The Core Value Prop (read this first)

Find My Mahj Game is a free service that helps American Mahjong players find local people to play with. We run a player matcher and a "Bench" of substitutes so a game never falls apart when someone cannot make it, and we help people discover nearby teachers, venues, and events. We are starting in Texas and growing out from there.

What makes us different and easy to say yes to:

- We are free. Free for players, free for partners. No fee, no contract, no catch.
- Money never crosses the table. We do not take a cut of anything, we do not sell players' data, and we never collect cash at the game.
- We bring people to you. You give them a friendly place to gather, or a fun, low-cost activity for your group. We do the organizing.
- We are safe by design. Games meet in public places. We publish a venue's public name and address, never a player's home address or phone number. First names only.

Three one-line pitches, pick the one that fits the audience:

- For a venue: "We send you mahjong players during your slow hours. You give them a welcoming place to play. No money changes hands."
- For a JCC: "We help your members find regular mahjong games and bring new players through your doors, at no cost to you or them."
- For a company or group: "We make it easy to run a fun, social mahjong session for your team or group. We handle the teaching and the organizing."

Quick facts you can share if asked (all true, never embellish):

- American Mahjong is a 152-tile game played by four people at a table. It is social, mentally engaging, and beloved across generations.
- All levels can play together. Beginners learn fast, and experienced players are always happy to welcome someone new.
- The National Mah Jongg League releases a new card each year in the spring, so there is always something fresh to learn.

A note on honesty: we are a brand new platform. We do not claim member counts or testimonials we do not have. We lead with what we do and the value we bring, not with numbers we cannot back up. Fill in real local details before you send.

## Part 2: Venues (libraries, senior centers, community centers, cafes, clubs)

Venues are places where four people can sit and play for two to three hours. Libraries, senior centers, and community centers are the fastest yes because their rooms are built for older adults and their staff want good programming. Cafes and clubs work when they have genuinely slow hours to fill.

### The value prop (venue)

You get happy, regular foot traffic during your quiet hours, a standing reason for people to come in, and a community activity you did not have to build. We bring the players and do the organizing.

### The ask (venue)

- A table that seats four (or a few tables) and a quiet-enough corner for two to three hours.
- A standing day and time we can rely on, ideally a weekday slot during your slower hours.
- A friendly staff contact who will let regulars in and mention the game to your members.

That is the whole ask. No fee, no contract. Either side can pause with a week's notice.

## Part 3: JCCs (Jewish Community Centers)

JCCs are a natural home for mahjong. Many already run or have run a game, they have rooms built for groups, an engaged adult and senior membership, and program staff who actively look for activities that fill the calendar and bring members in. Treat a JCC like a premium community partner: warm, member-first, and easy.

### The value prop (JCC)

We help your members find regular mahjong games and we bring new and lapsed players back through your doors. If you already run a mahjong game, we help fill empty seats so it never falls apart when someone cannot make it. If you do not run one yet, we help you start one with very little work on your side. All free.

### The ask (JCC)

- A room with tables that seat four, on a standing day and time (a weekday slot works well).
- Permission to list the game so members and the wider community can find it.
- A program contact who can mention it in your newsletter, calendar, or members' email.

If you already host a game, the ask is even smaller: let us help fill open seats and list your existing game so more players find it.

The session is social, hands-on, and easy for total beginners. You run it; we make it simple.

A quick note on what this is and is not. At launch, the offer is a free, founder-run introductory session and ongoing help finding players and venues. It is a relationship and community play, not a paid corporate package. If a group later wants a larger or recurring program, we can talk about what that looks like. Lead with the free, friendly version.

### The value prop (corporate / group)

A mahjong session is a genuinely fun, low-pressure way for a group to connect. No athletic ability, no awkward ice-breakers, just four people at a table learning a beautiful game together. It works across ages and skill levels, beginners included, and people remember it. We bring the know-how so the organizer does not have to.

- A date, a room, and tables that seat four (one table per four people).
- A rough headcount so we can plan tables and welcome everyone.
- An internal contact to coordinate with and to help spread the word to the group.

## Part 7: Common Questions and Honest Answers

Keep these answers ready. Always tell the truth, and never invent numbers or testimonials.

- "Is there really no cost?" Yes, free. Free for you and free for the players. We do not take a cut of anything and no money changes hands at the game.
- "What is the catch?" There is none. We are building the place where mahjong players find their people, and a free, friendly game at your location helps players and helps you. That is the whole model.
- "Do we have to sign a contract?" No. We start with a handshake and a confirmed day and time, and either side can pause with a week's notice.
- "Will you share our members' information?" Never. We publish your public venue name and address only. We never share a player's home address or personal phone number, and we use first names only in anything public.
- "Do players need experience?" No. All levels play, beginners included. We welcome new players and pair them up so no one feels lost.
- "How big can this get for us?" A game can start at one table of four and grow to several tables as word spreads. We help it grow at whatever pace suits your space.
- "Could there ever be a paid option?" Down the road we may offer an optional way for a venue that wants extra promotion to be featured. That is later and entirely optional. The game is and stays free to host.

## Part 8: How to Work This Kit (founder's quick guide)

1. Build a short list. For each audience, list five to ten real local targets with a named contact and an email. Start with the warmest relationships and the places nearest your launch market.
2. Pick the template that matches the audience exactly. Venues, JCCs, and corporate / group each have their own.
3. Fill every {{token}}. Never send a message with a blank or a bracket showing. Read it once out loud before you hit send.
4. Send in small batches so you can reply quickly when people answer.
5. Follow up once or twice, politely, if you hear nothing in 3-4 business days.
6. The moment you get a yes, send the matching confirmation from Part 6 and put the date on your calendar.
7. Keep a simple tracker (name, organization, audience, date sent, status, next step). Status values: Not started, Sent, Replied, Call booked, Confirmed, Hosting, Passed.

One rule above all: lead with what helps them, keep it warm and plain, and remember that money never crosses the table. That promise is your strongest pitch.
