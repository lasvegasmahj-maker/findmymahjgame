---

## Who a Champion is (and who is not)

A Champion is the warmest, highest-leverage tier of the Founding Ambassador program. Not every ambassador is a Champion. Use this tier only for people who clearly pass all three tests.

A Champion:
1. Already brings players together. She runs a class, a club, a standing game, or a circle of friends who play. She does not need us to find her first four people.
2. Wants to do it. She is eager and available now, not someday.
3. Is trusted in her town. When she says "come play," women come.

If a person has reach but no time, or warmth but no players to draw on, they can still be a wonderful ambassador. They are just not a Champion. Keep this tier small on purpose. A Champion gets the founder's direct attention, so we can only carry a handful at launch.

The one number that matters: tables formed, then filled, then played, then recurring. A Champion only counts when real games happen. Recruit for that.

---

## What we ask of a Champion

Keep it small and specific. We ask for three things in the first 30 days.

1. Start one real table. Pull together a foursome from people she already knows, pick a public place, pick a regular day and time, and get the first game on the calendar. We set up the table page for her.
2. Open one game to her town. Turn one of her sessions into an open play that any nearby player can join, listed on the site. This is how new women in her area find her.
3. Bring one new person in. One player, one teacher, or one venue she introduces us to. A warm hand-off, never a hard sell. We do the follow-up.

That is the whole ask. She supplies the people and the local truth. We do the coordinating, the writing, and the heavy lifting.

We do not ask her to manage schedules, chase no-shows, share anyone's contact information, sell anything, or be good with technology. If she can send a text, she can do this.

## What a Champion gets

Everything below is real today. We never promise a feature we do not have.

- First in her city. She is the founding face of mahjong in her town, recognized by name.
- A personal welcome from the founder. A numbered Founding Ambassador note in Shauna's voice that says, plainly, you were one of the first.
- Her own referral code and link. Every table started through her link is credited to her, automatically. She tracks nothing by hand.
- The founder on call. A direct line to Shauna and the team. Real answers, fast, from a person.
- Her own community served. Her students get games to join, her members get partners, her events get reach. This sends beginners back to teachers and players back to venues. It feeds her work, it does not compete with it.
- Early access to new tools as they ship.
- A real say in how mahjong grows in her city.

One honest note for later: ambassador profiles and public visibility are coming, and Champions go first when they do. We do not promise a date and we do not pretend they exist yet.

What it costs: nothing. Free for her, free for every player. We are not selling anything to her group, and money never crosses the table.

## Common questions, honest answers

Use these in conversation or in writing. Keep them plain.

- "Will this take a lot of time?" No. Most of the work is you sharing a link with players you already know. The site does the scheduling and coordinating. I do the listings and follow-up.
- "Is it really free?" Yes. Free for every player and free for you. We are not selling anything to your group, and money never crosses the table.
- "I am not very technical." That is fine. Everything runs from a phone in a few taps, and I will walk you through it on a short call.
- "Will this compete with my class or my space?" The opposite. It sends beginners back to teachers and players back to venues. You become the local hub.
- "What do I actually get?" You are first in your city, recognized by name, with your own referral code, a direct line to me, and more games for your own community.
- "Is it safe for the women I send?" Yes, by design. Players give only a first name and a phone or email. Home addresses are never published or shared. New groups meet in a public place for the first game.

## Activation: the first 30 days

This is how a signed Champion becomes a producing one. Run the milestones in order. Each one makes the next easier. Table, then open game, then referral. Never push ahead of a step that is not done.

### Step 1. First table (days 1-7)

The play: she starts one table and fills it from people she already knows. We build the table page so a real seat exists to claim.

Her steps:
1. We set up her table page at findmymahjgame.com/start on a 10-minute call and hand her the link.
2. She picks a public spot in her town: a library meeting room, a community center, a senior center, or a cafe with table space.
3. She picks a regular day and time.
4. She forwards the link to 6-8 friends or classmates. She needs only 3 to say yes.

Ready-to-send nudge:

> Subject: Your first table is one message away
>
> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> Let's get your first table going this week. I already set up your table page, so there is a real seat for people to claim: {{table_link}}
>
> Here is all you do:
> 1. Pick a public spot in {{city}} (a library, community center, senior center, or a cafe with table space). I can suggest a few if you tell me your neighborhood.
> 2. Pick a regular day and time.
> 3. Forward your table link to 6-8 friends or classmates. You only need 3 to say yes.
>
> Reply with your neighborhood and your preferred day, and I will send you two or three specific venues to choose from.
>
> Warmly,
> {{your_name}}

If she stalls: no venue, we hand her 2-3 specific public spots in her neighborhood. Cannot fill seats, we add her table to /play so nearby players find it. Nervous about hosting, we co-host the first session by phone or pair her with a nearby ambassador.

### Step 2. First open game (days 7-14)

The play: she opens one of her sessions to drop-ins and we list it on the site, so new women in her area can find her.

Her steps:
1. Pick one regular session to open to nearby players, marked all levels welcome or beginners welcome.
2. Tell us the date, the venue, and whether beginners are welcome. We write and publish the listing the same day.

Ready-to-send nudge:

> Subject: Let's put your group on the map
>
> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> Your table is rolling, so here is the next easy win. Let's open one of your {{city}} sessions to nearby players. This is how new women in your area find you.
>
> The simplest version: once a month, open one regular session to drop-ins and mark it all levels welcome. I will create the listing for you. Just tell me the date, the venue, and whether beginners are welcome.
>
> Reply with those three things and I will have it live the same day.
>
> Warmly,
> {{your_name}}

If she stalls: frame it as opening one session she already runs, no new commitment. If her usual spot is too small, we find a public room (most branch libraries and senior centers have one). If she does not know what to list, we offer two ready templates (a monthly open play or a beginners teach-and-play) and write it for her.

### Step 3. First referral (days 14-30)

The play: she introduces one new person into the pipeline. A player who claims a seat, a teacher who agrees to talk, or a venue that agrees to host. It is strongest now, because she is inviting someone into something real, not a pitch.

Her steps:
1. Think of one person: a friend who wants to learn, a teacher she knows, or a venue that would welcome a regular game.
2. Make a warm introduction, or send the right link: /play to find a game, /start to start one, or hello@findmymahjgame.com to reach us.
3. Tell us who she sent, so we follow up and she gets the credit.

Ready-to-send nudge:

> Subject: Who is the one person you'd bring in?
>
> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> You have a table and an open game on the map. Here is the last step, and it's the one that grows everything.
>
> Think of one person who should be part of this: a friend who has wanted to learn, a teacher you know, or a venue that would welcome a regular game. Just reply with their first name and how to reach them, or send them my way at hello@findmymahjgame.com, and I will take it from there. You get the credit, I do the work.
>
> Warmly,
> {{your_name}}

If she stalls: prompt with categories (a neighbor, a former student, your instructor, a cafe you like) rather than asking for a name cold. Make it a hand-off, not an ask: she gives the name, we make the approach. She never has to sell.

### The weekly founder routine

Block 60-90 minutes the same day each week, ideally Monday. Read every Champion's status. Send each one exactly one nudge for her current milestone, never two at once. Do the hand-offs you promised the same week (build table pages, write listings, follow up on referrals). Log status and referrals in the CRM. Pick the one Champion who has gone quiet and call her, do not email.

The recovery ladder: no movement in 7 days, send a small, do-it-for-her nudge for the exact milestone she is stuck on. No movement in 14 days, call her. Always shrink the ask. Always offer to do it for her.

### Activated, and the real win

A Champion is Activated when she has a filled table, an open game on the map, and one referral made, all inside 30 days. The deeper win is an Anchor: her first table meets a second time. A table that meets twice is a habit, not an event. Track Activated Champions, with Anchors as the signal underneath.
