# Activation Workflow: From "Yes" to First Game to Freshness Loop

What happens the moment a contact (a teacher, an open-play host, a venue, a group organizer) says "yes, list me." This is the founder's runbook. It turns a yes into a claimed, live, confirmed listing and a first real game, then drops the contact into the freshness loop so the listing never goes stale.

This is built and live, not aspirational. The claim links, the claim page, the edits queue, and the freshness loop all exist in the product today. Where a step is a founder action, it says so. Where the site does the work, it says so.

Plain-language promise to every contact: it is free, money never crosses the table, and a real person reviews any change before it appears. Lead with what helps them; their students and players find games, and they get listed as a trusted host.

---

## The five stages at a glance

| Stage | What it is | Who acts | Time target |
|-------|-----------|----------|-------------|
| 1. Listing created | The listing exists in the system, approved and public | Founder (in /admin) | Same day as the yes |
| 2. Claim link sent | Their personal magic link goes in a warm email | Founder | Within 24 hours of the yes |
| 3. Listing claimed | They confirm it is theirs, fix any details | The contact (on /claim) | Within a few days |
| 4. First game confirmed | A real game happens, or a real next-game date is set | Founder + contact | Within 14 days |
| 5. Freshness loop | They get periodic "still running?" asks; the listing stays fresh | The site + founder | Ongoing |

Activated is the goal, not listed. A listing that nobody claims and that never produces a game is not a win. The win is a claimed, confirmed listing with a first game on the calendar.

---

## Stage 1. Create the listing (founder, same day as the yes)

Goal: the listing exists and is public before you send the claim link, so the link lands on something real.

1. Open /admin and find the listing, or add it. Teacher and host inventory lives in the venue and event listing tables. If the contact is new, enter them now: business or host name, city, state, a short description, the public website or registration link, and a public contact only. Never a home address; use a neighborhood, a ZIP, or a public venue.
2. Verify the links before you publish. Any website or registration URL must load (expect a working page). The admin tool flags dead links and holds them out of bulk approval. Do not publish a row with a dead link.
3. Approve it. In /admin, approve the single row (or include it in a reviewed batch). Approved means status published and the listing is now public.
4. One honesty check. Nevada teachers are never published as competing teachers (the Las Vegas guardrail). If this is a Nevada teacher, keep the record but do not publish it as a public teacher listing.

At the end of Stage 1 the listing is live and findable. Now make it theirs.

---

## Stage 2. Send the claim link (founder, within 24 hours)

Goal: hand the contact a personal link that lets them confirm the listing is theirs and fix anything you got slightly wrong.

How to get the link:
1. In /admin, on the listing row, use "copy claim links." This calls the system and returns a personal claim link for that exact listing. The link is signed and good for 90 days, because this audience reopens email for weeks.
2. The same action also produces two freshness links (a "still running" link and an "ended" link) in the browser console. Save those two with the contact's record now; you will use them in Stage 5. You do not send them yet.

Paste the claim link into a short, warm email in your own voice. Do not send a system blast; the personal note is the highest-ROI founder action of the whole sequence.

The claim email (copy, paste, personalize):

> Subject: Your Find My Mahj Game listing is ready
>
> Hi [first name],
>
> Thank you for saying yes. Your listing is live on Find My Mahj Game, free, so players in [city] can find you.
>
> Will you take 60 seconds to confirm it is yours and fix anything I got wrong? Here is your personal link: [paste claim link]
>
> It opens to your listing. Check the details, edit anything that is off, add your email so I can confirm changes with you, and tap the button. A real person (me) reviews any change before it appears, so nothing goes live that you did not approve.
>
> Once it is confirmed, I will help you get your first game on the calendar. Any trouble at all, just reply to this email or call me.
>
> So glad you are here.
> Shauna

Notes for the founder:
- One link per listing. Do not reuse another contact's link.
- If they say the link expired (after 90 days) or it will not open, reply and use "copy claim links" again to send a fresh one.
- Replies to this email come to you. Watch for them.

## Stage 3. The contact claims the listing (the contact acts; founder reviews edits)

Goal: the contact confirms the listing is theirs and the details are correct. This is also the first signal the listing is genuinely active.

What the contact does (on the /claim page their link opens):
- Sees their listing pre-filled.
- Edits anything that is off (name, city, description, website, day and time, and so on).
- Adds their email so you can confirm changes with them.
- Taps "Confirm my listing."

What the system does automatically:
- Records the claim against that listing (one claimer per listing).
- Stamps the listing as confirmed active right now. A claim alone proves the listing is alive, so the freshness clock starts here.
- Sends any proposed edits to the founder's edits queue. Edits never change the public listing directly; they wait for your approval.
- Emails you that the listing was claimed (and tells you if edits are waiting).

What you do when the claim email arrives:
1. If there are no edits, nothing is required; the claim confirmed the listing is active. Send a one-line thank-you and move to Stage 4.
2. If edits are waiting, open /admin/edits, read the change, confirm it is real and safe (no home address, links load), and approve or decline. Approved edits go live; the contact is told when it is live.

Edge cases:
- "It says already claimed." Two emails for the same person, or a genuine conflict. Anyone who hits this is told to email hello@findmymahjgame.com, and a real person (you) sorts it out.
- A typo in a link they enter is skipped, not saved as blank, so a bad keystroke never erases their real website.

At the end of Stage 3 the listing is claimed, correct, and freshly stamped. Now turn the listing into a game.

---

## Stage 4. Confirm the first game (founder + contact, within 14 days)

Goal: a real game happens, or a real next-game date is on the calendar. This is the "graduation" moment that the rulings call the highest-ROI founder action within 14 days of every claim. A claimed listing that never produces a game is only half activated.

This stage is a phone-or-text relationship motion, not a product feature. Reach out the way that contact responds best.

1. Pick the path that fits the contact:
   - A teacher or host with an existing class or group already has players. Help them point those players to their listing and lock a standing day and time.
   - An open-play or venue host already has a recurring session. Confirm the next real date and make sure the listing shows it correctly.
   - A host who needs players to come together can start a table at findmymahjgame.com/start (a day, a time, a general area, four seats, they take the first). When the fourth seat fills, the site emails everyone first names and the general area, recommends a public place for the first game, and routes replies to the host. New groups are guided to meet in public (a library, community center, cafe, or senior center) for the first game.
2. Offer to do it together. Many contacts are warm, capable people new to the tool, and many are in their 60s or 70s. The single most effective move is "give me 15 minutes by phone and we will have it set before we hang up." Make the offer explicitly.
3. Confirm the first game. Get either a game that actually happened or a specific next-game date you can hold them to. Log the date.
4. Make the path to recurring clear. After the first game, the goal is a standing game (same day, next week). A standing game is the difference between a nice afternoon and a real community. The product supports starting the same table again; the founder's job is to offer it.

First-game nudge (text, copy and personalize):

> Hi [first name], your [city] listing is confirmed, lovely. Let us get your first game on the calendar this week. Do you have a day and time that already works, or would 15 minutes on the phone be easier? I will help you set it up.

When the first game is set, celebrate it briefly. A short, genuine "you did it" reinforces the win and earns you the next conversation about making it recurring.

At the end of Stage 4 the contact has a confirmed first game or a real next date, and they know the path to a standing game.

---

## Stage 5. Add to the freshness loop (the site + founder, ongoing)

Goal: the listing stays true. Nothing erodes trust with a 60-to-85 audience faster than a player driving to a game that no longer exists. The freshness loop keeps every listing honestly current.

How freshness works in the system today:
- Every claim and every confirmation stamps the listing "confirmed active" with a date. Search ordering can favor confirmed-recent listings, so fresh listings serve players better.
- You already saved two links for this contact back in Stage 2: a "still running" link and an "ended" link. Those are the freshness loop. They are private, signed, and good for 90 days.
- The "still running" link re-stamps the listing as active. The "ended" link records that the listing may be over and emails you on the first report so you can verify before it disappoints a player. The system is bounded so a replayed link cannot spam you or inflate reports.

The freshness rhythm (founder motion):
1. On a regular cadence (a roughly six-week check is a sensible default; tighten it for time-sensitive open plays), email each claimed contact a short "are you still running?" note with their two saved links.
2. If they tap "still running," the listing re-stamps itself active. No further action needed.
3. If they tap "ended," or if you get an ended report, verify it. If the listing is genuinely over, unpublish it in /admin so no player is sent to a dead game.
4. If a contact goes silent across a couple of cycles and the listing is going stale, decide: a personal call to re-confirm, or unpublish. A stale published listing is worse than no listing.

The freshness email (copy and personalize):

> Subject: Quick check, is your [city] game still running?
>
> Hi [first name],
>
> A quick one so players always find the truth on Find My Mahj Game. Is your game still running?
>
> Still going: [paste "still running" link]
> It has ended or paused: [paste "ended" link]
>
> One tap is all it takes, and it keeps your listing fresh and near the top for local players. Thank you for keeping it real for everyone.
>
> Shauna

At the end of Stage 5 the listing is either freshly confirmed or honestly retired. The contact stays in this loop for as long as they host.

## What the founder tracks per contact

Log these in the tracker so no claimed contact goes quiet:

- Date of the yes.
- Date the listing was published.
- Date the claim link was sent.
- Date claimed (claim confirmed).
- Date of the first confirmed game (target: within 14 days of the claim).
- Whether a recurring game was set.
- Last freshness confirmation date (and the next check-in date).

The single most important intervention: if a contact claimed but has no first game within about 14 days, a personal call is the highest-leverage move. Do not let a claimed listing sit without a real conversation.

---

## Failure points and what to do

- They never claim. After about a week, resend the claim link with a one-line, friendly nudge, then offer a phone call. The claim is what stamps the listing active and unlocks edits.
- The claim link expired. It is good for 90 days; after that, generate a fresh one from /admin and resend.
- Edits look wrong or risky (a home address, a broken link). Decline in /admin/edits and reply to ask for a correction. Nothing they submit goes live without your approval.
- "Already claimed" conflict. They are routed to email a real person; you reconcile which email is the true owner.
- The game never happens. Treat it as a relationship gap, not a product gap. Call, offer to set it up together, or start the table for them on /start.
- The listing goes stale. The freshness loop catches it. On an "ended" report, verify and unpublish if it is truly over.

---

## One-line summary

A yes becomes a published listing (Stage 1), a personal claim link in a warm email (Stage 2), a claimed and corrected listing that the system stamps active (Stage 3), a confirmed first game within 14 days (Stage 4), and a contact who stays in a light, ongoing freshness loop so the listing is always true (Stage 5). Listed is not the goal. A claimed listing with a real first game, kept fresh, is.
