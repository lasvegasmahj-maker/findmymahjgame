## bam_better
- Scale and incumbency on the club layer: Bam Good Time lists ~1,218 clubs across the US and Canada, the largest aggregated club inventory in the category, and even pre-aggregated 1,026 unique clubs across 51 states/territories in its 'State of American Mahjong 2026' report (sourced partly from Meetup). FMG launches with seed data, not real listings.
- Strongest SEO competitor: Bam ranks for high-intent local queries with a content farm of /clubs, /mahjong-clubs/[city], /tournaments, and blog pages. FMG has 50 state pages plus thin city seed data and has not yet earned that organic footprint.
- Real, defensible SaaS moat: Bam runs club-management software plus native apps that lock organizers in (a walled garden FMG cannot match at launch), and it monetizes via SaaS, tile sales, and apps rather than relying on a single free flow.
- A funded competitor can simply buy the supply FMG refuses to pay for: cash can outbid FMG's 'social currency' with the scarce teachers and hosts, sponsor classes, and buy the spring card-season moment with paid acquisition, neutralizing FMG's organic, concierge approach.
- Proven ability to aggregate ~1,000 listings as one team, meaning Bam is already in the land grab and could extend from clubs toward player/table discovery before FMG reaches density.

## fmg_better_vs_bam
- Owns the truly unowned intent: 'find a 4th / find an individual player near me.' Bam confirms on its own site it has NO individual-player directory; it is club-centric, so a solo player cannot find a single game or seat there. FMG's player_listings flow is built precisely for this.
- Free and neutral, with the 'money never crosses the table' promise, versus Bam's club-SaaS-and-products monetization and walled-garden native app that locks organizers in.
- Designed for the atomic unit Bam ignores: the standing four-person Table and player-to-Table matching (claim/request a seat, find-a-sub-this-week), not a static club list. That is a two-sided network effect Bam's club software does not produce.
- Mahjong-native, granular filtering Bam does not offer at the player level: level (beginner/casual/serious), day/time, NMJL current-card vs learning, stakes, house rules, venue type, and women-only host preference.
- Person-first, women-45-to-70-friendly framing (belonging and relief, safety via public-venue-first and group-as-buffer) rather than Bam's organizer/club tooling orientation, which serves a different buyer.

## ilm_better

- A genuine, active online community with virtual groups, plus a teacher directory that already ranks for 'find a mahjong teacher' and 'learn mahjong.'
- Recurring subscription revenue from online play plus paid lessons, a durable monetization engine versus FMG's free model.

## fmg_better_vs_ilm
- Solves in-person 'near me' play, which is I Love Mahj's blind spot: ILM is online-first and its groups are virtual, not geographic, so it never seats a real woman at a real local table this week. FMG's entire job is intent-to-seat in person.
- Texas-first plus city/neighborhood-level, location-indexed, Google-crawlable pages capture local 'find players near me' demand that ILM's online platform does not target.
- Free and explicitly non-paid (no subscription, money never crosses the table) versus ILM's subscription-play and paid-lessons gating.
- Builds a real-world geographic network: standing Tables, hosts, venues, and a who-plays-with-whom trust graph in physical communities, a moat an online-play platform cannot replicate.
- Captures the highest-frequency, highest-retention in-person use cases ILM does not address: finding a 4th this week, drop-in open play near me, and seasonal new-card games forming locally.
